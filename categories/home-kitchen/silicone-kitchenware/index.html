2. Platinum-Cure vs Peroxide-Cure: The Material Truth

The single most common material fraud in silicone kitchenware sourcing is cure system fraud. Suppliers label their products "food-grade silicone" but use peroxide cure instead of platinum cure. The difference is invisible in appearance but directly determines food safety compliance, temperature resistance, and long-term durability.

3 Ways to Verify Platinum-Cure Before Mass Production

1. Odor + Heat Test (On-Site)

Warm a small sample piece with a heat gun to 150°C. Platinum-cure has almost no odor. Peroxide-cure emits strong rubbery chemical smell within 30 seconds. Quickest on-site check.

2. Density Test

Weigh a uniform small cube (e.g., 2×2×2 cm). Pure silicone = 1.1-1.2 g/cm³. Heavy filler content = 1.5-1.8 g/cm³. Recycled silicone almost always shows density > 1.4.

3. Third-Party Lab Test

Peroxide residue migration test + heavy metal migration. Costs $150-300/sample. Required for Amazon LFGB compliance documentation. Dongguan labs: ~¥800-1500/sample.

Reference standards: FDA 21 CFR 177.2600 (silicone rubber food contact), EU Regulation 10/2011 (plastic materials in contact with food), Germany LFGB §30 (silicone elastomers). Always verify on production samples, not just approval samples — suppliers often send genuine platinum-cure for approval but switch to peroxide-cure for mass production.

4. Factory Verification: The MSDS + Injection Mold Test

Silicone kitchenware factory verification is fundamentally different from stainless steel or knife factories. The critical question is not "do they buy raw material" (all injection molders buy compounded silicone rubber), but "what grade of silicone are they buying and do they own their mold tooling".

Factory Verification Checklist — Silicone Kitchenware
Platinum-Cure Confirmation (5 checks):
1. Ask for the raw silicone MSDS (Material Safety Data Sheet)
2. Check silicone rubber brand: Dow Corning / Wacker Chemie / Jiangsu Hongda (reputable)
3. MSDS must list platinum catalyst (not peroxide/benzoyl)
4. Verify the buyer name on the MSDS matches supplier business license
5. Confirm factory does NOT also produce non-food silicone (indicates filler/recycled usage)
Real Factory vs Trading Company:
• Factory owns injection molding machines (not desktop presses)
• Factory can show you their mold tooling inventory
• Factory does NOT produce both silicone AND plastic AND metal
• Factory can confirm LFGB test reports in their own name
• If they can't show mold photos, they're a trading company

Key red flag: If a supplier quotes platinum-cure price but cannot show you the MSDS sheet from a major silicone compounder (Dow, Wacker, Hongda), they are using peroxide cure or recycled silicone. Reputable factories buy compounded silicone (not raw PDMS) and keep MSDS sheets on file for every batch.

7. Common Pitfalls & Scams

1. Cure System Fraud (Peroxide → Platinum Label)

The #1 silicone scam — saves 30-50% on raw material. Counter: MSDS verification + heat odor test + third-party lab peroxide residue test.

2. Recycled Silicone Mixed In

Factories mix 20-40% post-industrial silicone grind into 'food-grade' compound. Counter: density test (1.1-1.2 = pure, 1.4+ = filler/recycled), ask for MSDS from batch.

3. Fake LFGB / FDA Certificates

Factories often show certificates from a different product batch or another supplier entirely. Counter: verify report number on the issuing lab's official website (e.g., TÜV SÜD, SGS).

4. Sample Approve → Mass Produce Scam

Supplier sends genuine platinum-cure for approval, then switches to peroxide for mass production. Counter: lock platinum-cure spec into contract, specify MSDS check on first production box.

5. Trading Company Posing as Mold Factory

Suppliers claim to be silicone injection molders but are actually trading companies buying from a Dongguan factory. Counter: ask for injection molding machine photos + mold inventory photos.

6. Low Ball Then Quality Crash

Supplier quotes platinum-cure price, accepts your order, then produces peroxide-cure or filler-heavy silicone at last minute to hit margin. Counter: include material grade specification + AQL acceptance before final payment in contract.

Q: What's the difference between platinum-cure and peroxide-cure silicone?
A: Platinum-cure uses a platinum catalyst — zero odor, -60°C to +230°C, LFGB compliant. Peroxide-cure uses organic peroxide — rubbery chemical smell, up to +180°C, cannot pass LFGB. Platinum-cure costs 30-50% more per kg. 40-50% of 'food-grade' silicone from China is peroxide cure labeled platinum.
Q: How do I verify silicone kitchenware is actually food-grade?
A: (1) Ask for raw silicone MSDS sheet — must show platinum catalyst (not peroxide/benzoyl). (2) Heat a sample to 150°C — platinum-cure has almost no odor. (3) Third-party lab peroxide residue test ($150-300). (4) Density test — pure silicone = 1.1-1.2 g/cm³; filler-heavy = 1.4+ g/cm³.
