How to verify us before you send a single dollar — license, transparency and references.

Ask for three things any real China sourcing agent can show within 24 hours:
  • Business license (营业执照) with a Unified Social Credit Code you can check yourself on the National Enterprise Credit Information Publicity System (gsxt.gov.cn)

✅ Quality Control

Three QC gates on every order — with dated photos and functional test videos.

Three gates:
  • ① Pre-production — golden sample and spec confirmation, raw material check
  • ② During production — in-line check when 20–30% of the run is completed
  • ③ Pre-shipment — AQL 2.5 sampling inspection: appearance, function, quantity, packaging, plus drop tests where relevant
Functional tests are recorded on video so you see the product working, not just a checklist.
Yes — every inspection includes dated photos, functional test videos and a written report with a defect list graded by severity (critical / major / minor). You approve the shipment, or the factory reworks first. Nothing ships without your sign-off.
The approved golden sample is sealed and kept at our warehouse; the pre-shipment inspection compares bulk units against it side by side. On mismatch, the factory reworks or replaces at their own cost per the purchase contract — which is exactly why we never release your balance before QC passes.
AQL (Acceptable Quality Limit) 2.5 is the consumer-goods inspection standard. Example: for a 500-unit order we inspect 50 units (General Inspection Level II) and the lot passes with up to 3 major defects; for 501–1,200 units we inspect 80, passing at up to 5 majors.
Insider tip: an agent quoting "100% inspection" on a large order at no extra cost is usually sampling and calling it 100%. Always ask for the AQL standard and sample size in writing.
Report within 14 days of delivery with photos: we verify against the inspection records and negotiate replacement, refund or credit with the factory. Defect liability is written into the purchase contract before production starts, so the factory can't argue it away.
Always. The sequence is: 30% deposit → production → QC pass + your report approval → 70% balance → ship. Your money is the leverage that makes factories fix problems fast — we structure every order so the leverage stays on your side until the goods are proven good.

US sea DDP: 28–40 days door-to-door (West Coast faster, East Coast +5–7 days); air DDP 8–12 days. EU sea 30–45 days; rail 18–25 days. Add a 3–5 day buffer in peak season (August–October) and around Chinese New Year, when factories and ports slow down for 2–3 weeks.
DDP (Delivered Duty Paid) covers: pickup from the factory, export customs, ocean/air freight, import customs, duties and taxes, and last-mile delivery to your address or FBA warehouse. One price, no surprise bills on arrival. The only possible extras are seller-side changes — e.g., a shipping-plan change or address correction after dispatch.

⚠️ Avoiding Pitfalls

The scams we see every week — and how to make sure they never happen to you.

The six we see reported most often:
  • ① Too-good pricing — deposit paid, "supplier" disappears
  • ② Golden sample, junk bulk — sample perfect, mass production downgraded
  • ③ Secret supplier switch — your agent quietly moves to a cheaper factory
  • ④ Photoshopped 1688 payment proofs — you're billed more than the agent paid
  • ⑤ Fake freight invoices — padded CBM or invented surcharges
  • ⑥ Packaging upcharges — 3–5× the printer's real price
All six share one fix: verifiable invoices plus independent QC before the balance.

Related guide: Yiwu Market Scams to Avoid →

Run this test: ask for the factory's 1688 link or business license "for your compliance records". If the agent refuses or stalls, a markup is likely. Then compare your unit price with similar 1688 items — an unexplained gap above 20% is a flag. Finally, ask for the forwarder's rate sheet behind your freight quote.

The seven that cost the most money:
  • ① Skipping samples to "save time"
  • ② Paying 100% upfront
  • ③ No written spec sheet — "same as the photo" is not a spec
  • ④ Ignoring compliance documents until customs holds the goods
  • ⑤ Ordering 1,000 units of an unvalidated product
  • ⑥ Comparing quotes on price only while the spec quietly creeps down
  • ⑦ No pre-shipment inspection
The single fix covering most of them: a two-page spec sheet plus QC before the balance.
