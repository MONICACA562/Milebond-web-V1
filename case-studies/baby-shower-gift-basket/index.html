One reference photo in, 9 measured parts and 5 avoided mistakes out.

How we quoted a baby keepsake gift basket kit for an Amazon seller — and what we told them they shouldn't buy.

How this category actually works

Baby keepsake baskets are usually sold on Amazon as two SKUs — one blue, one pink — for the same product. That makes the bows and the gender sign structural parts of the offer rather than decoration: if either one runs out, a whole variant cannot ship.

Cost is driven by two things, not nine. The basket sets the CBM, and CBM is what sea freight prices; the flowers decide how much protection the rest of the kit needs. Printed parts — the insert card and the sign — are a small share of the unit cost, which is why we spend more time on basket construction than on artwork.

Two constraints decide most of the design choices in this category. An assembled gift set that exceeds 18 inches on its longest side leaves Amazon’s standard parcel tier and starts paying dimensional weight. And natural woven grass needs a fumigation certificate before export, while paper rope and plastic do not. Both constraints shaped the five recommendations below.

What the client sent us

The client — an AMZ customer — came with two reference photos — a woven basket with the lid closed, and the same basket open with bows, a clip for an ultrasound photo, and artificial flowers. No tech pack, no drawing, no supplier contact.

They asked us for six things:

  • A water hyacinth basket with a removable liner
  • Pre-tied satin bows in blue and pink, for boy and girl variants
  • A raised-lettering “Boy” / “Girl” sign
  • An artificial flower arrangement
  • Clips with rope to hang the ultrasound photo
  • Retail packaging, assembly and FBA prep

They also asked us to quote three alternative options they were considering: a foldable basket, a PP/PE plastic rattan-look basket, and a PVC sign. That is a normal RFQ — most agents would simply price all of it. We didn’t.

We quoted three volume tiers — 250, 500 and 1,000 sets. The factory cost per set falls as volume rises; our fee stays the same percentage on every tier; and the forwarder’s DDP quote passes through at cost. What the shipping company charges is what the client pays.

But the BOM was only half the email.

The other half was what we told them not to buy — and why. Five things we raised proactively, even though nobody asked.

Five things we talked this client out of

PUSHBACK 1

Skip the foldable basket.

Client asked: “Can you do a foldable basket to save shipping space?”

Three reasons we said no:

1. A foldable basket costs more per unit, not less — the hinged structure adds material and labour.

2. Laid flat it is still 20.5″ × 6.3″, over Amazon’s 18″ standard parcel limit, so dimensional weight fees apply anyway. No shipping saving.

3. The artificial flowers still need crush protection when the basket folds, so the “foldable saving” doesn’t exist.

→ Client kept the rigid basket. No oversize FBA fees.

PUSHBACK 2

Try paper rope, not plastic rattan.

Client asked: “Also quote a PP/PE plastic rattan-look basket. Does that avoid fumigation?”

We didn’t just quote the plastic option — we suggested a third path they hadn’t considered: paper rope.

Same unit price as plastic, better fit for a baby gift (natural, non-plastic), and no fumigation certificate needed — which removes the wood-packaging inspection fee from every shipment.

We still quoted the plastic option so they could compare all three constructions with full data. We didn’t hide the option they didn’t ask for — we recommended it.

→ Client is comparing all three. Paper rope is the lead option.

PUSHBACK 3

Don’t do the full-colour gift box on a 250-unit trial.

Client asked: “Price both a full-colour gift box and a branded kraft box.”

The full-colour gift box adds a per-unit premium and carries a 500-unit MOQ. On a 250-unit trial, half the box order becomes dead stock if the product doesn’t sell.

Our recommendation: start with the branded kraft box, which runs at 250 pcs, and upgrade to full-colour once the product proves out. The trial order also costs less.

→ Client agreed to the kraft box for the trial order.

PUSHBACK 4

MDF sign, not PVC — unless volume justifies the mould.

Client asked: “Besides MDF, what would acrylic or PVC signs cost?”

PVC with raised lettering and a raised border needs a custom injection mould — a four-figure tooling fee, plus a high MOQ. MDF gives the same painted, laser-cut, raised-border look with no tooling cost.

We told them plainly: we’ll revisit PVC when volume justifies the mould. There is no point paying for tooling on a product you haven’t launched.

→ Client agreed to MDF. No mould cost on the first order.

PUSHBACK 5 — THE ONE NOBODY ASKED ABOUT

What’s your target Amazon retail price?

Nobody asked us this. We raised it ourselves at the end of the quote email.

“May I know your target sales price on Amazon for this set? So I can research whether there is another way to optimise the cost — mostly on the basket. This is a big CBM item, so the shipping fee is high.”

This is the part most agents skip. They quote what you asked, send the invoice and wait for payment. We want to understand your margin maths before you commit — because if the landed price doesn’t support your retail target, the order doesn’t work for either side. Better to find that out on day one than after production.

→ Client shared their retail range. We are exploring alternative basket constructions to hit their target landed cost.

What else we were honest about

Beyond the five pushbacks, three things most agents leave out of a quote.

On samples — we don’t call them “free”

We didn’t write “free samples!” like most agent homepages. We told them the truth: the pre-production sample is charged at vendor cost — we can’t guarantee every supplier waives it, and we won’t eat the cost and bake it into the bulk price. The mass-production sample is 1 set free, deducted from the bulk order.

On DDP pricing — we don’t guess

The client asked for DDP to Amazon FBA by sea and air. We didn’t invent a number: “DDP price depends on final weight and volume, and on destination, so we can’t final-quote it yet. What the shipping company charges is what we charge you.” No markup, and no DDP figure that turns out 15% higher when the forwarder bill lands.

On payment terms — we don’t pretend to be a big enterprise

First order: 50% deposit, 50% before shipment. Most of these components aren’t off-the-shelf — they need secondary processing, custom sizing and custom printing, across seven factories. The deposit covers our vendor deposits; the balance is due after QC and before the container leaves. We said that plainly, and noted that reorder terms can flex as the relationship grows.

Questions buyers ask about this quote

The same answers we gave the client, including the parts we recommended against.

Is a foldable gift basket cheaper to ship than a rigid one?

No. A foldable basket costs more per unit because the hinged structure adds material and labour. Laid flat it still measures about 20.5 × 6.3 inches, over Amazon’s 18-inch standard parcel limit, so dimensional weight fees apply anyway. The flowers inside still need crush protection, so the shipping saving does not exist.

Does a PP or PE plastic rattan basket avoid fumigation?

Plastic avoids the fumigation certificate, but so does paper rope — and paper rope has the same unit price as plastic while fitting a natural baby-gift theme better. Paper rope removes the wood-packaging inspection fee on every shipment.

Should a first trial order use a full-colour gift box?

Usually not. A full-colour gift box adds a per-unit premium and carries a 500-unit MOQ, so on a 250-unit trial half the box run becomes dead stock. A branded kraft box runs at 250 pcs and costs less on the trial; upgrade to full colour after the product proves out.

MDF or PVC for a raised-lettering gender sign?

MDF for a trial order. PVC with raised lettering needs a custom injection mould costing a four-figure tooling fee plus a high MOQ. Painted, laser-cut MDF delivers the same look with no tooling cost; revisit PVC only when volume justifies the mould.

What should a sourcing agent ask before quoting a gift set?

The target retail price. Without it the landed cost cannot be tested against the margin, and a quote can look workable while the product never sells. Milebond asks for the target Amazon or retail price before committing, then optimises the biggest cost driver — usually the basket and its CBM.

How long does a quote like this take?

48 hours from the first email for a nine-part set like this one, including the factory cost per line and the three volume tiers. Complex categories with tooling or compliance testing take longer, and we say so upfront rather than quoting blind.
