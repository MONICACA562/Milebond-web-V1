# Case study template — problem → action → result

Every new case study follows this structure. Numbers first, adjectives last.

## 1. Title (H1)

Put the outcome in the headline, with at least one number.

- Good: `A False 304 Claim, a 51% Cost Cut, and Net 30 Terms`
- Bad: `How we helped a client source better`

## 2. KPI band (4 cards, right under the title)

Four hard numbers a buyer can scan in 3 seconds. No number, no card — use "unchanged" instead of inventing one.

- Cost / MOQ / lead time / compliance / payment terms
- If a metric genuinely did not move, say so ("$1.00, unchanged"). It builds more trust than a fake improvement.

## 3. The problem

Two paragraphs maximum.

- What was broken, in concrete terms (defect, delisting, cost, deadline)
- The cost of the problem in money or time (`$18,000/month`, `listing delisted`)

## 4. What we did

Two paragraphs maximum. Only actions a buyer can verify.

- Who did what, for how long (`our QC engineer spent 7 days on the floor`)
- The specific mechanism (material re-test, supplier swap, packaging redesign)
- No "we leveraged synergies" language

## 5. The result

A before → after table. Same rows as the KPI band, plus one process metric.

| Metric | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| MOQ | 1,000 pcs | 300 pcs (-70%) |
| Unit price | ¥11 | ¥5.40 (-51%) |

## 6. Client quote (optional but preferred)

One sentence, in the client's voice, about the outcome — not about how nice we are.

## 7. Compliance note

Always include: `Client identity and ASINs are anonymized. No client or supplier names are disclosed. Where a figure did not change, it is shown unchanged.`

## 8. CTA

`Sourcing a similar product?` + `Get a Free Quote` + `See Our Fees`.

## Rules

- Never publish a number you cannot point to a document, invoice or report for.
- If the only result is "compliance fixed at the same price", make that the headline — it is still a result.
- Keep the whole page under 1,200 words.
