2. 304 vs 201: The Material Truth

The most common scam in stainless steel kitchen utensil sourcing is material fraud. Suppliers label products as "304 stainless steel" but use cheaper 201, 430, or even electroplated iron. The difference is invisible to the naked eye but dramatically affects durability, rust resistance, and food safety compliance.

Property304 Stainless Steel (Genuine)201 Stainless Steel (Common Substitute)Electroplated 304 (Worst Case)
Chromium content18-20%16-18%Base metal may be 0%
Nickel content8-10.5%1-1.5% (or none)Only in plating layer
Rust resistanceExcellent (indoor use)Moderate (rusts in humid/salty env)Poor (plating wears off, base rusts)
Food contact safetyFDA / LFGB compliantMay fail heavy metal migration (Mn)Fails once plating chips
Material cost (per kg)~¥18-22/kg~¥10-14/kg~¥5-8/kg (base metal)
Magnetic test resultNon-magnetic (or weakly magnetic after forming)MagneticMagnetic (base metal)

3 Ways to Verify 304 Before Mass Production

1. Test Solution ($2-5)

Drop a stainless steel identification solution on the surface. 304 stays unchanged or turns light yellow; 201 turns red/pink within 30 seconds. Must scrape surface first to bypass plating.

2. Spectrometer ($50-100)

Handheld XRF spectrometer gives exact Cr/Ni/Mn composition. 304 must show Cr ≥18% and Ni ≥8%. Most reliable method. Can rent or hire a testing lab in Yiwu for ~¥200/sample.

3. Salt Spray Test

5% NaCl salt spray for 48 hours. Genuine 304 shows no rust; 201 shows rust spots within 12-24 hours. Best for final batch verification. Yiwu testing labs charge ~¥300-500/sample.

Reference standards: ASTM A240/A240M (304 composition), ASTM A959 (201 composition). Always verify material on the actual production sample, not just the supplier's provided sample — suppliers often send a genuine 304 sample for approval but use 201 for mass production.

4. Factory Verification: Raw Material Cost Test

The most reliable way to distinguish a real factory from a trading company is the raw material cost proportion test. A real factory that produces stainless steel utensils will have stainless steel sheet/coil as its largest cost component. A trading company cannot show you material purchase invoices because they don't buy raw material — they buy finished products.

Raw Material Cost Proportion Test — Stainless Steel Utensils
For a genuine stainless steel factory:
• Raw material (SS sheet/coil): 55-70% of total cost
• Labor: 10-15%
• Energy/equipment: 5-8%
• Packaging: 3-5%
• Overhead/profit: 7-17%
Verification steps:
1. Ask for the material purchase invoice (not a proforma invoice)
2. Check the invoice is from a steel mill (e.g., TISCO, Baosteel, Tsingshan)
3. Verify the buyer name on the invoice matches the supplier's business license
4. Cross-check: material cost ÷ quoted price should be 55-70%
5. If below 40%, it's almost certainly a trading company
Key principle: A real factory cannot produce both stainless steel AND plastic AND textile products. If a supplier's catalog covers multiple unrelated materials, they are a trading company or aggregator. For stainless steel utensils, the factory should only produce stainless steel products.

7. Common Pitfalls & Scams

1. Material Fraud (201 as 304)

Supplier sends genuine 304 sample for approval, then uses 201 for mass production. Always test material on production samples, not just approval samples.

2. Trading Company Posing as Factory

Supplier claims to be a factory but is actually a trading company. Use the raw material cost test (Section 4) and verify business license manufacturing scope.

3. Low Ball Quote Then Price Increase

Supplier quotes unrealistically low to win the order, then increases price after deposit or at final payment. Always get 3+ quotes and be suspicious of prices 30%+ below market average.

4. Hidden Kickbacks from Agents

Some sourcing agents accept 5-15% kickbacks from suppliers, which is built into your price. Ask for transparent fee structure and written confirmation of no kickbacks.

5. Packaging Not Optimized for FBA

Supplier uses oversized packaging that increases FBA fees and shipping cost. Always calculate dimensional weight and optimize packaging before mass production.

6. No Pre-Shipment Inspection

Buyer pays balance before inspection, then receives defective goods. Always do pre-shipment inspection (AQL standard) before paying the balance.
