2. Borosilicate vs Soda-Lime: The Glass Type Truth

The most common fraud in glassware sourcing is glass type mislabeling. Suppliers stamp 'borosilicate' on soda-lime or tempered glass to fetch premium pricing. This is dangerous — soda-lime glass cracks at 150°C while borosilicate safely handles 400°C, meaning counterfeit borosilicate bakeware can shatter in the oven and cause injury.

4 Ways to Verify Borosilicate Before Mass Production

1. Thermal Shock Test (On-Site)

Heat sample to 200°C in oven → 5 min → plunge into ice water. Borosilicate survives. Soda-lime cracks/shatters within 30 sec. Quickest and safest on-site check (but broken sample glassware costs money).

2. Refractive Index Test

Use a refractometer (cost ~¥3,500 in China). Borosilicate = 1.474, soda-lime = 1.512. Instant reading, non-destructive. Most reliable on-site quantitative test.

3. Spectrometer (Boron Oxide Content)

XRF spectrometer tests B2O3 percentage. Genuine borosilicate = 12-15% B2O3. Costs ¥200-400/sample at Zibo lab. Most authoritative test, required for LFGB compliance.

4. Price Benchmark

1L 'borosilicate' carafe under $3.00 FOB = soda-lime. Genuine borosilicate melting + pressing minimum cost is ¥12-18/kg glass + ¥6-10 pressing = minimum ¥2.00 raw material cost per 1L carafe.

Reference standards: ASTM C169 (glass coefficient of expansion), GB/T 15726 (China national standard for borosilicate glass cookware), EN 12150-1 (tempered glass European standard). Always verify on production samples — suppliers send genuine borosilicate for approval, then switch to soda-lime for mass production.

4. Factory Verification: The Boron Oxide + Melting Batch Test

Glass factory verification is fundamentally different from stainless steel, silicone, or knives. For borosilicate, the critical question is "does this factory operate its own borosilicate melting furnace?". Trading companies pose as borosilicate factories constantly — they buy pressed soda-lime glass from Fengyang, stamp 'borosilicate' on it, and ship at premium pricing.

Factory Verification Checklist — Borosilicate Glass
Real Borosilicate Factory Confirmation:
1. Ask for boron oxide purchase invoices (not glass cullet)
2. Check supplier name matches invoice → Borax / Yiteng / Zibo Boric
3. Verify melting batch logs showing B2O3 12-15% content
4. Request photos of their continuous melting furnace
5. Confirm factory is located in Zibo (not Fengyang)
6. If they show only pressing equipment + cullet piles → they're a trader
Trading Company Red Flags:
• Cannot show boron oxide invoices
• Located in Fengyang but claims 'borosilicate'
• Sells both soda-lime AND borosilicate AND plastic
• Has LFGB certificate but not in their name
• Cannot explain the B2O3 percentage in their glass
• Pressing machines but no melting furnace photos
⚠️ 40-60% of Alibaba 'Zibo borosilicate' suppliers are traders

Key principle: A real borosilicate factory MUST operate its own melting furnace (minimum 5-ton continuous melting = ~¥3M investment). Trading companies never run melting furnaces — they buy pressed cullet and repackage. The Fengyang → Zibo scam is the most common: Fengyang soda-lime traders pose as Zibo borosilicate exporters.

7. Common Pitfalls & Scams

1. Glass Type Fraud (Fengyang → Zibo Scam)

Fengyang soda-lime traders pose as Zibo borosilicate exporters. Counter: verify supplier has boron oxide invoices AND is located in Zibo.

2. Recycled Cullet Heavy Metal Leak

Factories mix 50-80% recycled bottle glass (may have lead paint residue) into soda-lime batches. Counter: LFGB heavy metal migration test on final product.

3. Fake LFGB / FDA Certificates

Factories present reports from 3+ years old, wrong product batch, or another supplier entirely. Counter: verify lab report number on issuing lab website (TÜV SÜD, SGS, INTERTEK).

4. Sample Approve → Mass Produce Scam

Supplier sends genuine borosilicate for approval, switches to soda-lime for mass production. Counter: lock borosilicate spec + B2O3 12-15% into contract, specify refractometer check on first production box.

5. Under-Tempered Glass (CE Fake)

Tempered glass must produce ≥ 40 fragments per 50×50mm. Many factories produce only 20-30 fragments, too dangerous for safety. Counter: EN 12150-1 lab test on sample.

6. Low Ball Then Cracking

Supplier quotes borosilicate price at soda-lime level, accepts order, then blames "production mistake" and increases price 50% before delivery. Counter: 0.5% per day penalty clause + glass type spec in contract.
