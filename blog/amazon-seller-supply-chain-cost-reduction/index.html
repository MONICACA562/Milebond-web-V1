Strategy 2: Quality Control That Actually Improves Reviews

02

6-Stage QC Process

What We Did

Replace "inspect at the end" with "prevent every step"

  • 1Material verification — test composition, thickness, color BEFORE production starts.
  • 2Pre-production sample approved and locked in as quality standard.
  • 3In-line inspection at 10-20% completion to catch defects early.
  • 4AQL 2.5 final inspection — you approve the report before we ship.
  • 5Packaging inspection — FNSKU, barcodes, box strength verified.
  • 6Container loading check — random carton opening, quantity confirmed.
Result: Negative reviews dropped by 60%. The product review rating climbed from 4.4 to 4.7 in 90 days because defective units never reached Amazon customers.

Strategy 4: FBA Packaging & Logistics Engineering

04

Packaging That Saves You Money

What We Did

Cut shipping cost by 18% without breaking boxes

  • 1Re-engineering box dimensions to reduce DIM weight by 30% (critical for Amazon FBA).
  • 2Eliminated unnecessary bubble wrap — used corner protectors only where needed.
  • 3Optimized carton count per FCL — fit 12% more units per container.
  • 4Set up warehousing near FBA warehouses for faster, cheaper last-mile delivery.
Pro tip: Amazon charges by DIM weight, not actual weight. A 0.5kg item in a 40x30x25cm box bills as 6kg. We reduced one seller's box to 28x22x18cm and saved $1.80/unit on inbound — before changing the product at all.

AQL (Acceptable Quality Limit) 2.5 is an international statistical sampling standard used for final pre-shipment inspection. From a batch of, say, 500 units, we randomly pull 50 and inspect them rigorously for appearance, dimensions, function, material composition, and packaging. If fewer than 2.5% of sampled units fail, the batch passes. If it exceeds the threshold, the factory must rework or replace the defects at their cost — not yours.
