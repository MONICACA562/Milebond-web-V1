Why Agents Fake Payment Screenshots (The Math)

There are two versions of this scam:

  1. The complete ghost scam: Agent creates a fake supplier page, sends a screenshots of a fake order, collects your deposit, and vanishes. Typical loss: $3,000–$15,000.
  2. The markup scam: Agent actually does place the order on 1688 — but for 20–40% less than they told you. They show you a fake, inflated screenshot to make you think the factory charged more than it actually did. Typical overcharge: $500–$4,000 per order.

The second version is far more common because the agent doesn't have to disappear. They just need to Photoshop the "Total Paid" number higher.

What a REAL 1688 Order Detail Page Looks Like

A genuine order detail page (订单详情) on 1688 has these non-negotiable markers:

  • A green Trade Assurance (买家保障) badge at the top — this is the single most important indicator. Fake pages never render this correctly.
  • A six-digit transaction progress timeline (pending → paid → shipped → confirmed) with timestamps
  • The supplier's store name is a clickable link that takes you directly to their 1688 storefront — not just static text
  • Payment details show method (Alipay, corporate transfer), exact amount, and 3–5 minute precision timestamp
  • The bottom shows logistics tracking with a carrier code and tracking number

5 Forensic Detection Methods

These five checks catch 98% of faked 1688 payment screenshots in our internal audits.

1. The Pixel Mismatch Check

Open the screenshot in any photo tool (even Preview on Mac or Paint on Windows). Zoom to 200–300%. Look at the "Total Paid" amount specifically.

Fake numbers are pasted as new text. The fonts never perfectly match 1688's default (it's a custom Alibaba sans-serif). You'll see:

  • Slightly different letter spacing
  • Subtle color difference — the real font is a specific gray (#66686C on the current 1688 theme), while pasted text often lands on a nearby gray
  • A thin, slightly darker edge or halation around the pasted digits — Photoshop selection artifacts
Test it: Compare the amount font to the order number font in the same screenshot. On a real page, both use identical weight, color, and spacing.

2. The Font Consistency Check

1688's order pages render all numbers in a single CSS-declared font (PingFang SC for Chinese, Alibaba PuHuiTi for new versions). If one number block is noticeably bolder, taller, or narrower than another, someone pasted it in.

Quick hack: Use the Eyedropper tool on the exact color of two different numbers. If the hex codes differ by more than #10 in any channel, it's a fake.

3. The Rounded-Number Test

Real 1688 prices are almost never clean round numbers at the final paid amount. Listing prices end in .07, .85, .56. Shipping is rarely a whole dollar. The total sum — especially in RMB — ends in random cents.

If you see a "Total Paid: $4,000.00" — red flag. Real payments look like "¥28,347.62" or "$3,914.17".

4. The URL Bar and Clickable-Link Test

A real screenshot taken from a browser will show a URL bar containing 1688.com/order/detail.htm?orderId=XXXXX. Many scammers crop this out or use a fake URL bar drawn in.

Ask the agent to send you the order ID as text, then open it yourself by typing https://trade.1688.com/order/detail.htm?orderId=THEIRID in a private browser window. If you can't see the order — or see a completely different amount — you have your answer.

5. The Direct-Supplier Cross-Reference

This is the nuclear option, and it catches everything. Ask the agent for the 1688 store URL and the salesperson's direct WeChat. Add the factory's sales contact yourself, and ask:

"Hi, I'm working with [Agent Name] as a buying agent. Can you confirm: my order for [product, quantity] was placed on [date] for approximately [RMB amount]?"

Factory salespeople are busy, but they will confirm or deny. Agents will almost never let you do this — if they stall, insist. Your money is on the line.

FAQ

My agent sent an order screenshot — should I pay more to "verify" it?
Never pay a fee for someone to verify an order. 1688 order detail pages are partially public — copy the order ID into the 1688 URL and check it yourself in a private browser tab. Zero cost, one minute.
1688 is in Chinese — can I view order pages in English?
Alibaba has an English 1688 mirror at 1688.com (it auto-redirects). Order detail pages, Trade Assurance badges, and payment timestamps all render in English there. The URL trick works the same.
What if my agent says "I can't show you the order — the factory requires confidentiality"?
That is a lie. 1688 Trade Assurance rules give the buyer full visibility into the order. A factory cannot override that. If your agent refuses to show you the order ID, stop all payments immediately and file a dispute.
Can I get my money back after a fake payment scam?
Yes — if you paid by Alipay or a traceable corporate bank transfer. File through Alibaba Trade Safety (tradesafety.alibaba.com) and provide the fake screenshot as evidence. Success rate is about 60–70% within 3 months. Personal WeChat red envelopes have near-zero recovery.
