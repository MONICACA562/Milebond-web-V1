Agent Switched Suppliers: 6 Forensic Methods to Catch Them Red-Handed

Your agent showed you a premium factory, sent sample photos from their showroom, quoted you their price — then quietly swapped to a cheaper factory that pays the agent a 10–30% kickback. Here's how to prove it.

Get Free Supplier Audit
8 min read · Updated Sep 2026

You did your homework. You reviewed 5 factories with your agent, chose Factory A because of their ISO certification, their 10-year export history, and their premium unit price that felt like quality.

Then your agent placed the order — but actually placed it with Factory B. Much cheaper, much less reputable, but Factory B pays the agent a 25% cash kickback for every order they route their way.

This is the most damaging sourcing scam for product quality — and the hardest to catch unless you know the forensic checks. Most importers find out 3 weeks later when the shipment arrives and the quality doesn't match the sample.

In this article, we walk through the kickback math, then give you 6 specific forensic methods — each step-by-step — to prove your agent swapped suppliers before the goods even leave China.

Why Agents Switch Suppliers (The Kickback Math)

Let's formalize this so it's clear. Here's a real kickback structure we uncovered during an audit in 2026:

ScenarioFactory A (Premium, Approved)Factory B (Cheap, Kickback)

That's why agents do it. The incentive is overwhelming. For a $3,850 order, the agent can make 6x more money by lying to you about which factory they used — and you'll pay the same invoice either way. If you don't audit, you simply never know.

6 Forensic Detection Methods

These six checks work before shipment — meaning you can catch the switch, demand the correct factory, or get a full refund before you pay for freight.

Method 1: The Factory Sign Video

This is the single most powerful check, and it's almost impossible to fake. Ask your agent to send a 10-second horizontal video of the goods being packed or inspected in front of the factory's physical sign — the outdoor metal sign visible from the street with the factory name on it.

Why this works:

  • Every Chinese factory has a large outdoor metal sign with their registered company name on it — required for business licenses
  • These signs are unique physical objects — you can Google image search the factory name + "工厂大门" (factory gate) to verify it matches
  • Agents can't borrow another factory's sign for a video — it would require physically transporting workers and goods there, which isn't practical

Script: "Can you send me a quick video of today's production at Factory [X] — maybe a worker next to the factory sign with our product batch? Just 10 seconds."

Method 2: The Name-in-Box Trick

This is our favorite because it's verifiable 100% of the time. Tell your agent: "Before packaging, can you have the factory write a random Chinese character (e.g., 风, meaning 'wind') on the outside of 3–5 boxes with a marker, and send a photo?"

When your shipment arrives, open it and check for the character. If it's there — good. If not — you were packed at a different factory, and your agent can't explain why the character you requested isn't on the boxes.

This is fail-safe. Agents have no way to know you'll ask for a specific character, and the wrong factory won't have been briefed to include it.

Method 3: The Shipping Origin Address Check

Factories have geographical clusters. If your approved factory is in Shenzhen, Guangdong, and your goods ship from Yiwu, Zhejiang — that's a 700 km red flag. Here's how to verify:

  1. Get the forwarder's tracking number from your agent before shipment
  2. Enter the tracking number on the carrier's website (DHL.com, FedEx.com, etc.)
  3. Look at the first origin scan — it will show a city and province
  4. Cross-reference with the address on your factory's 1688 storefront

Even if the agent switches factory, they can't move a shipping origin address. Goods from Zhejiang factories come out of Ningbo. Goods from Guangdong come out of Shenzhen or Guangzhou. These hub differences are structural.

Method 4: Approved Sample Comparison

You should have a physical sample from the approved factory. When the pre-shipment photos arrive from your agent, compare the goods in the photos to your physical sample using these five markers:

  • Material color consistency: Take both photos outdoors in natural light — not indoors under warm LEDs. Even 5% color difference is visible.
  • Stitching/glue bead thickness: Chinese factories have characteristic glue application or stitching patterns. Two different factories will never match exactly.
  • Label positioning: Each factory's assembly line applies labels with a specific 2–3 mm tolerance range. If the label is 8 mm left on one sample vs. 15 mm left in the production photo — different factory.
  • Packaging bag film thickness: Hold the sample polybag and the production photo's bag description up to a light. Cheap factories use thinner film.
  • Serial number/date code: Many factories etch a tiny batch code on the product. If you requested "batch Q3-2026-04" on the sample and production photos show a different code — different factory.

Save your sample. Keep it. These physical differences are impossible to fake in photos.

Method 5: Factory Sales Contact Verification

Here's the nuclear option for after-the-fact proof:

  1. Get the registered factory name from the 1688 storefront (click the store name → look for the "信用代码" or unified social credit code)
  2. Search for that company name + "WeChat" or "联系电话" on WeChat or Baidu
  3. Add the factory's sales manager directly on WeChat
  4. Send: "Hi [Manager Name], I'm a client of [Your Agent's Name]. Did you receive an order from them for [Your Product] on [Date]? My order ID was [XXX]."

If the factory never heard of your agent or your order — you have your answer. Factory sales managers are busy, but they will reply yes or no. This is admissible evidence for Alibaba Trade Assurance disputes and PayPal chargebacks.

Method 6: Photoshopped Supplier Name Detection

We covered this in our fake invoice article, but it bears repeating: agents sometimes edit the supplier name on the 1688 order page to match your approved factory's name. Check:

  • Pixel mismatches in the factory name — zoom to 300%, compare font color and spacing to surrounding text
  • Store link mismatch — click the supplier name on the 1688 page; does it actually go to the approved factory's storefront? Real 1688 pages make store names clickable links.
  • Factory location in order detail — the order detail page shows factory location. If it says "Yiwu" and your approved factory was "Shenzhen" — fake.

What to Do Immediately When You Suspect Switching

If you find evidence your agent switched suppliers, move fast:

  1. Freeze all pending payments. Do not pay the remaining 70% balance until this is resolved.
  2. Send a formal email + WeChat message to your agent stating what you found and demanding an explanation within 24 hours. Cite specific evidence (factory sign mismatch, factory sales manager's reply, etc.).
  3. File a Trade Assurance dispute on Alibaba.com if you used Trade Assurance — evidence from the approved factory's 1688 storefront vs. the actual factory's storefront is extremely strong.
  4. Contact the actual factory directly. Factory B (the cheap one) will often confirm the kickback arrangement if you ask — they hate bad agents too because it risks their reputation on 1688.
  5. Charge back via your credit card or PayPal if you paid by those methods. "Goods not as described" is a valid chargeback reason for supplier switching.

Preventative Checklist for Future Orders

Do these things before you pay any agent — they cost nothing and make supplier-switching impossible:

  • Get the 1688 order URL from your agent the day they place the order. Open it in a private browser. Click the supplier name — does it go to your approved factory's storefront?
  • Request the factory sign video as a non-negotiable in your contract or service agreement with the agent.
  • Pick a random "secret marker" (character, symbol, sticker) and tell your agent to have the factory apply it before packaging. Verify it on the pre-shipment photos.
  • Get the factory's WeChat contact upfront — during supplier selection, not after order placement. Establish a direct relationship so you can cross-check freely.
  • Use Alibaba Trade Assurance in your own name — this gives you direct rights over the order, not through the agent.

FAQ

Can I check the factory location on 1688 order pages?
Yes. The 1688 order detail page (订单详情) has a factory location field in the "供应商信息" section. It shows city and province — e.g., "东莞市 Dongguan City." Compare this to your approved factory's location on their storefront.
What if the two factories are in the same city?
Then the shipping origin trick doesn't work — but the factory sign video, name-in-box trick, and sales contact verification still do. Same city means different signs, different factory gates, and different sales managers.
How long does Alibaba Trade Assurance dispute take for supplier switching?
Typically 7–15 business days. Alibaba's Trade Safety team investigates both the agent's order evidence and the factory's responses. If you have the factory sales manager's direct WeChat confirmation that they never received the order, you're looking at ~90% win rate.
My agent says the factory "subcontracted" to another supplier — is that legitimate?
Rarely. Chinese factories almost never subcontract their own production — they're set up vertically. "Subcontracted" is 95% a euphemism for switching to a cheaper factory. If a factory truly can't fulfill an order, they'll tell the agent and ask to push delivery — not silently route it to another factory.
