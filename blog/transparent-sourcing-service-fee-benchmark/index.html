2026 Transparent Sourcing Service Fee Benchmark: What Should You Actually Pay?

We surveyed 47 agents across Yiwu, Guangzhou, and Shenzhen. Here's what they actually charge — and why the "3%" agents are the most expensive ones to work with.

"We charge just 3%."

If you're shopping for a China sourcing agent, you've heard this. Maybe from an agent in Guangzhou. Maybe from a freelance guy on Fiverr. It sounds like a steal — until you receive your first invoice and realize the "3%" is on top of a factory price that's been marked up 15%.

This is the year we stop playing that game. We surveyed 47 sourcing agents across Yiwu, Guangzhou, and Shenzhen — fee-only, commission-based, and full-service — and compiled a transparent benchmark. Here's what buying from China should actually cost in agent fees.

The Problem With "%" Fees

A percentage fee tells you almost nothing. The critical question is: percentage of what?

  • Fee-only (transparent): Percentage of the actual factory invoice price. You see the factory's invoice. 5% of that is the agent's only revenue.
  • Commission-based: The agent gets a kickback from the factory (usually 10–20%) on top of whatever they charge you. Their "3%" fee is their public commission; the real cost is 3% + 10–20% hidden.
  • Markup-based: The agent quotes you a unit price that includes their margin. You never see the factory's actual price. They'll call it a "3% fee" but they mean 3% of their already-marked-up price — plus their markup is invisible.
The dirty secret of the industry: Agents advertising "3%" are almost always either commission-based or markup-based. The fee-only agents — the ones where 5% is the only revenue they earn from your order — almost never quote below 4%. Because there's a cost to doing this properly.

These agents show you the factory invoice and charge a flat percentage on top. This is the smallest group — only 11 of the 47 we surveyed.

Group 2: Commission-Based Agents

These charge the buyer a "fee" (typically 2–4%) AND receive a kickback from the factory (10–20%). The visible fee is low, but the total cost is higher.

  • Visible fee: 2–4%
  • Hidden factory kickback: 10–20%
  • Effective total cost to buyer: 12–24% of the factory invoice

Group 3: Markup-Based Agents

These quote unit prices that already include their margin. Some claim "no fee" at all. You'll see "5% fee" on top of a factory price that's been inflated by 10–25%.

  • Stated fee: 3–6%
  • Hidden markup: 10–25%
  • Effective total cost to buyer: 13–31%

At 5%, the agent makes $500 on a $10k order — but their operating cost is $650–$1,300. They lose money on small orders unless their fee covers it. This is why every legitimate fee-only agent either has a higher percentage for small orders or a minimum fee.

How to Verify a Fee Is Truly Transparent

Here's a 4-point test you can run on any agent in 5 minutes:

1. Ask to see the factory invoice before payment

A transparent agent will show you the original Chinese factory invoice (with supplier name, unit price, and total). Their fee is clearly marked on a separate line item.

A non-transparent agent will say: "We'll show you after you pay" or "The price we quote is all-inclusive."

2. Ask: "Do you receive any payment from the factory?"

Fee-only agents should answer "no" — and put it in writing. Commission-based agents will hesitate or say it's "industry standard."

3. Ask who pays for the QC inspector

If the agent says "the factory pays" — that's a kickback. QC should be an independent expense paid by the buyer or the agent (out of their fee), never by the factory.

4. Compare two quotes for the same product

Get quotes from a "3%" agent and a 5% fee-only agent for the identical product from the identical supplier. The fee-only agent's total landed cost will almost always be lower — because their factory price is lower.

5 Red Flags That a Fee Isn't the Only Revenue

  1. "We don't mark up the product" — but they won't show the factory price. If they truly don't mark up, showing the invoice is risk-free. Their refusal is the proof.
  2. "We have exclusive relationships with 1,000+ factories." Exclusive = the factory pays them to route orders. You're paying that kickback.
  3. "3% flat fee on all orders, no minimum." See our cost breakdown above — this is mathematically impossible for a legitimate operation. Either they're making it up elsewhere or cutting critical corners (no QC, no warehouse, cheap suppliers).
  4. "We also handle shipping at cost." Shipping is where most non-transparent agents make their real margin. "At cost" is almost never actually at cost.
  5. The agent pushes you toward one specific supplier. If they only show you one option, it's likely that supplier pays them a commission. Fee-only agents show you 3–5 options and let you choose.
