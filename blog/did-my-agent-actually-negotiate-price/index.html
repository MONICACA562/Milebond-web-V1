Did My Agent Actually Negotiate the Price? The Chat Log Authenticity Checklist

You paid your agent a commission to get you a better factory price. But did they actually negotiate — or just copy the 1688 listing and mark it up? Here's how to check.

Get Free Price Audit
9 min read · Updated Sep 2026

Here's a quiet truth nobody tells you about sourcing agents:

Their commission structure gives them an incentive to not negotiate.

If an agent earns 10% commission on the product cost, and they negotiate the factory price down by 15%, their commission also drops by 15%. Every dollar they save you is a dollar they don't make — unless they layer a hidden markup on top of the already-negotiated price, which is exactly what many do.

We've audited enough WeChat chat logs to know: most agents skip negotiation entirely, copy the 1688 listing price, add their margin, and forward it to the client as "negotiated." This article walks you through how to spot it, what real negotiation chat logs look like, and how to do the negotiation yourself in 5 minutes if you need to.

The Commission Conflict of Interest (A Primer)

Before we dive into the checks, let's formalize the math so it's clear why this happens. A typical 1688 sourcing flow:

  • 1688 factory lists a product at ¥28.00 / unit ($3.87)
  • Client asks agent to source 2,000 units
  • If agent doesn't negotiate: They quote ¥28.00 + 15% markup = ¥32.20 ($4.46). Agent revenue: ¥8,400 markup.
  • If agent negotiates genuinely: They push factory to ¥22.00 ($3.04, 21% discount) for volume. Then they could quote ¥22.00 + 15% = ¥25.30 ($3.51). Agent revenue: ¥6,600 markup.

Genuine negotiation costs the agent ¥1,800 or ~22% of their revenue. That's the conflict. Agents have to choose between "do a good job for the client" and "maximize their own paycheck." Most choose the second.

The structural fix: Agents should be paid a flat project fee, not a percentage of product cost. We use flat fees at Milebond — because a 15% commission on a $1,000 save is backwards aligned incentives.

7 Signs Your Agent Didn't Actually Negotiate

Run through this checklist on your next order.

1. The Quoted Price Matches the 1688 Listing Price (Exactly)

Open the 1688 product page and look at the price grid. Most factory listings show tier prices: ¥28.00 for 100–499 pcs, ¥24.00 for 500–999 pcs, ¥20.00 for 1,000+.

If you're ordering 2,000 units and your agent quotes the ¥28.00 / unit for 100–499 tier — or even the ¥24.00 mid-tier — they did zero negotiation. The factory already offers volume discounts on the listing page! Negotiation should push you below the bottom-tier listing price.

2. No Evidence of Tiered Pricing Being Applied

Real negotiation chat logs show the agent explicitly referencing the volume-tier discount on the 1688 listing and asking for more. Example of a genuine agent chat log with the factory:

Agent (WeChat): "老板, 我客户要 2000 件, 你页面上 1000件以上是 ¥20, 能不能给 ¥18 做首单?"

Factory: "18 太低了, ¥19 行不行? 加上 FOB 运费 ¥0.8/件。"

Agent: "OK, ¥18.5, 我们给 ¥3k 定金。"

Agent references the listing's tier price, pushes down, confirms. That's real negotiation. If your chat log shows the agent just saying "客户要 2000 件, 多少钱?" and the factory replying "¥28" — no, that's not negotiation.

3. No Factory Contact Information Provided

If your agent refuses to give you the factory's WeChat contact, they are hiding something. The 1688 supplier has a direct sales contact (the store manager's Wangwang ID is on the storefront). Any genuine agent relationship includes the agent introducing the client to the factory sales rep, not gatekeeping them.

4. The Quoted Price Is Higher Than What Other Agents Quote

This is a sanity check, not proof. Ask two other agents the same product and quantity. If one agent consistently quotes 15–25% lower across multiple products, the higher one is not negotiating — they're copying listing prices and adding fat margins.

5. Chat Logs Don't Mention Your Specific Quantity

Take a close look at the WeChat screenshots your agent sends. Does the factory actually mention "2,000 units" specifically? Or is it a generic "好的, 价格优惠" ("OK, we'll give a discount") without a number attached?

Generic "discount" language in chat logs — especially without the specific quantity being negotiated — is a red flag for fake negotiation. Real Chinese factory sales teams anchor every quote to an exact volume.

6. No Reference to Ex-Factory (EXW) vs Delivered Price

Genuine negotiation always separates EXW Yiwu/Ningbo price from the additional costs (domestic trucking, packaging, export docs, etc.). If your agent quotes one bundled "product price" with no breakdown of EXW vs extras, they're inflating the EXW number and hiding the structure.

7. The Chat Timestamp Doesn't Match the Order Date

Agents sometimes resell old chat logs from previous orders. Check the timestamps. If the "negotiation" chat log is dated 2 months before your actual order, the agent reused a previous conversation and just inserted your quantity.

How to Negotiate with the Factory Yourself (Quick Script)

If you have the factory's WeChat or Wangwang ID, you can run a 5-minute negotiation yourself. Most factory reps speak basic English or have a co-worker who does. Here's the script we use for clients who want direct access:

You: Hi, I'm [Your Name] with [Your Company]. I'm looking at product [SKU/link on 1688] — I need 2,000 units, shipping to [Country]. Your listing shows ¥20/unit at 1,000+. Can you beat that for 2,000?

Factory (likely reply): Hello, 2000 pcs — ¥19 is best. EXW Yiwu.

You: ¥18.50 if we confirm within 24 hours. First order, but we'll reorder if this works.

Factory: OK, ¥18.50. Please send delivery address.

That's a typical flow — 5 minutes back and forth. The goal is to get the factory's direct, honest price, which you can then compare to what your agent quoted. If your agent's "negotiated" price is higher than the factory's direct reply, you know the agent didn't actually negotiate.

A Canadian home goods client, Jordan, approached us in June 2026 with a quote from a UK-based agent: silicone baking mats, 1,500 units at $4.20/unit ($6,300 product total + $630 agent commission = $6,930).

We located the exact 1688 supplier the agent had used (Jordan had the product link). 1688 listing tier price for 1,000+ was ¥27.80 = $3.84/unit.

Jordan added the factory sales rep on WeChat directly. Here's what the factory replied — verbatim — in English: "For 1500 pcs, we give you $3.20/unit EXW. If agent, they get 10% of factory price."

What to Do If You Find Fake Negotiation

Three steps:

  1. Get the factory's real quote first. Use the script above to get a direct number from the factory in writing. This is your evidence.
  2. Confront the agent with the difference. Most will capitulate when faced with a factory's direct WeChat screenshot and offer to refund the overcharge.
  3. Stop using commission-based agents. Move to a flat-fee agent, or negotiate directly with 1688 using Trade Assurance. Our main guide walks through the direct Trade Assurance setup step by step: How to buy from 1688 without an agent.

FAQ

My agent says "we've worked with this factory for years, they won't show their real price to a stranger." True?
False. 1688 factories are open to any verified buyer via Trade Assurance. Open your own 1688 account (takes 2 minutes) and you'll see all tier prices. If the factory genuinely won't give a price to you directly, that's a red flag they're cooperating with the agent to fake the negotiation.
What if the factory price looks higher than the agent's? Then the agent is doing a good job, right?
Possibly — but cross-check by asking the factory specifically for your exact quantity. 1688 listing prices often show higher minimums than you think. Ask: "For 2,000 units in one order, what's your best EXW price?" Don't compare the 100-piece tier to your 2,000-piece order.
I don't speak Chinese — how do I negotiate directly with the factory?
Google Translate works surprisingly well for WeChat text conversations. Copy-paste English → Chinese → back. Factory sales reps are used to this. For Wangwang (1688's built-in chat), Alibaba has a built-in translator. You don't need fluency, just the script above.
How much should I expect to save by negotiating directly vs. using a bad agent?
Typically 12–25% on the product price alone. When combined with avoiding shipping markup (see our shipping audit article), total savings range from $300–$3,000 per order depending on size.
Should I just go with the agent who gives the lowest quote?
Not automatically. Some low quotes are loss leaders to lock you in, then they mark up shipping/packaging. But if you find the lowest-quote agent also sends verified chat logs and the forwarder invoice — that's your agent.
