I Think My Agent Switched Suppliers: What to Do Next (Evidence + Recovery)

You paid for factory A's product with their specifications. The box that arrived says factory B on the packaging. Here's the step-by-step process we've helped buyers use to recover funds — including a real $8,000 recovery case.

The signs are hard to miss once you know what to look for:

  • The box says "Guangzhou XYZ Trading Co." but you ordered from "Yiwu ABC Factory."
  • The SKU number on the product doesn't match the spec sheet your agent sent.
  • The packaging is different — worse, usually — from the sample you approved.
  • You look up the actual manufacturer's address on the shipping documents and it's not where you expected.

Your agent switched suppliers without telling you. They quoted you based on the original supplier's price, then bought cheaper from somewhere else and kept the difference as profit.

This is not a misunderstanding. This is fraud, and it's surprisingly common. Here's exactly what to do.

Step 0: Stop Payments Immediately (Critical)

Before anything else:

  1. Do not pay the agent any more money. If you have an outstanding balance, put it on hold — send a clear email saying: "I am pausing all further payments until we resolve a discrepancy with supplier attribution."
  2. Do not sign any acceptance documents. If they send paperwork saying "goods received in good condition," don't sign it. Your signature waives any dispute rights.
  3. Alert your bank or card issuer. If you paid via credit card, file a pending dispute notice now. You have 60–120 days from the charge date for most card issuers. This preserves your chargeback rights even if you don't act immediately.
Time is critical. The longer you wait to preserve evidence, the harder recovery becomes. Do steps 0 today — even before you've fully confirmed the supplier switch.

Step 1: Preserve Evidence (Your Legal Foundation)

You need to build a documented, timestamped evidence trail. Here's everything to collect:

A. Photo and video comparison

  • Take photos/videos of the received goods showing all branding, SKU numbers, manufacturer marks, and packaging.
  • Compare side-by-side with photos of the original supplier's product you approved (from their 1688 page, Alibaba listing, or sample photos they sent you).
  • Take close-ups of the manufacturer label, country of origin stamp, and any batch/serial numbers.
  • Save all images with original timestamps (don't screenshot — use the originals).

B. Supplier identification

  • Look up the manufacturer name on the product packaging via Google Maps or 企查查 (China business registry search engine).
  • Cross-reference with the supplier information your agent provided. Are the addresses the same? Is the business registration number valid?
  • Search the actual manufacturer's products online. Does it match what you received?

C. Document your agreement timeline

  • Save all emails, WeChat messages, WhatsApp conversations where your agent named the specific supplier.
  • Save the original quote/invoice with supplier details.
  • Save shipping documents (bill of lading, commercial invoice, packing list) — these should state the actual shipper/manufacturer.

D. Contact the original supplier

This is the nuclear option but it's effective. Find the original supplier on 1688, Alibaba, or their own website. Send them a polite message:

"Dear [Supplier], We placed an order for [product] via our buying agent [Agent Name] on [date]. The goods we received show [Alternative Factory Name] as manufacturer. Could you confirm whether you produced and shipped our order? Our order reference number is [XXX]. Photos of the received goods are attached."

If they say they never produced your order — you have smoking-gun evidence your agent switched suppliers.

Step 2: The Confrontation Script

Don't bluff. Go in with your evidence and a clear demand. Here's a script that works:

Email / WeChat Message:

  • Subject: "URGENT: Dispute re: Order #[XXX] — Supplier Switch Confirmed"
  • Opening paragraph: State the facts (not accusations). "We have confirmed via [evidence] that our agent ordered goods from [Factory B] instead of the agreed [Factory A]."
  • List your evidence bullet-pointed.
  • State your demand clearly. Options include:
    • Full refund of the product difference (if B was cheaper)
    • Free replacement from the correct supplier
    • 100% refund of the order if the product is defective or different
  • Set a deadline (72 hours) for response.
  • State what you will do if they don't comply: file chargeback, report to Alibaba, report to their local business bureau.
Important tone note: Be firm but professional. Don't use profanity or threats beyond stating what you will actually do. Emotional rants make them less likely to engage reasonably.

Preventative Checklist (Do This Before Your Next Order)

The best recovery is never needing one. Do these with every agent going forward:

  1. Get factory invoice upfront. "Please provide the original factory invoice (Chinese version is fine) before I make payment." Transparent agents will have this ready. Non-transparent ones will make excuses.
  2. Require a factory visit report. "Please send 3–5 photos of the factory production line with our product before mass production starts."
  3. Lock in the supplier in your contract. "This order is specifically with [Supplier A]. Any supplier change requires written approval and a new quote."
  4. Independently verify the supplier on 1688. Look at their storefront, transaction volume, and ratings. Compare with what the agent quoted you.
  5. Use a third-party QC inspector. Hire an independent inspector (e.g., SGS, Bureau Veritas) to check goods before shipment. They'll identify if the product isn't from the right factory.
  6. Pay in stages: 30% deposit, 60% after QC photos, 10% after receiving and verifying. Don't pay 100% upfront.
