How to Compare Yiwu Sourcing Agents: 7 Critical Checkpoints for Amazon Sellers

JingSourcing, Supplyia, Leeline Sourcing and Milebond are built for different order patterns. Here is a factual, side-by-side look at the tradeoffs that decide whether a custom low-MOQ test batch makes money or eats your margin.

Get a Transparent Quote
11 min read · Updated Nov 2026

Search for a Yiwu sourcing agent and you will see the same names: JingSourcing, Supplyia, Leeline Sourcing. Most Amazon and DTC sellers start there, because those brands appear on recommendation lists, in YouTube reviews, and inside zero-fee sourcing ads.

The surface-level promise is simple: low or no fee, suppliers already vetted, consolidation and FBA prep handled. What those pages rarely show is the tradeoff underneath — how the agency earns its margin, what MOQ applies once your logo goes on the product, who actually performs the final inspection, and how long a real quote takes.

This article compares business models using public service facts. It is not a ranking, and it is not a claim that one agency is better than another. Each model is a reasonable fit for a specific buyer. The goal is to help you check whether you are that buyer before you send a deposit.

Most searches behind this article — JingSourcing alternatives, JingSourcing vs Supplyia, JingSourcing vs Leeline Sourcing — come down to the same three variables: what the fee is charged on, what MOQ applies to custom work, and who performs the final check before cartons leave China.

TL;DR: Buyers commonly see JingSourcing, Supplyia and Leeline Sourcing on recommendation lists. Many pick based on brand popularity or zero-fee ads, then run into four surprises: hidden markup, inflexible custom MOQ, outsourced final QC, and rigid standardized workflows. This guide compares JingSourcing, Supplyia, Leeline Sourcing and Milebond, and clarifies the tradeoffs for Amazon and DTC sellers testing custom branded low-MOQ SKUs.

1. Fee Structure & Transparency

Fee structure is where a comparison should start, because it determines who the agent actually works for. A percentage is only meaningful when you know the base it is charged on, and whether the factory price is visible.

JingSourcing: advertised zero fee, or 5% with a $100 minimum

JingSourcing publishes two pricing plans. The $0 embedded plan hides the factory price and builds the agency margin into the unit price. The 5% commission plan carries a $100 minimum. The company runs a large team, and that fixed overhead is baked into pricing. This is not a criticism of the model — it is simply a different revenue structure from a fee-only agency, and it shapes every quote you receive.

Supplyia: tiered commission inside packaged plans

Supplyia uses a 5–9% tiered commission and sells packaged startup sourcing plans. Factory invoice visibility is not guaranteed. Packaged plans simplify onboarding for new importers, but the all-in structure makes it harder to separate the sourcing fee from the product cost line by line.

Leeline Sourcing: custom project quotation

Leeline Sourcing quotes each project individually with no fixed public commission rate. The model is optimized for fulfillment and dropshipping with steady repeat volume. Project pricing can fit complex logistics, but there is no published fee benchmark to compare against another agent's quote.

Milebond: 3–8% of the factory invoice, USD 50 minimum

Milebond charges 3–8% of the factory invoice value with a USD 50 minimum fee. The core team is ten people, overhead is low, and large agency overhead costs are not passed to buyers. Original factory invoices are always shared, there is zero markup on the factory price, and the team takes no supplier kickbacks. The fee is the only revenue on the order.

Checkpoint test: A $0 sourcing plan is not free — the margin simply moves into the unit price. The useful question is not "what is the fee?" but "what is the factory invoice, and what sits on top of it?"

JingSourcing vs Supplyia is the comparison buyers ask about most often; the table at the end of this guide puts both next to Leeline Sourcing and Milebond in one view.

2. MOQ Flexibility for Custom Products

Stock-item MOQ and custom-branded MOQ are two different numbers. Recommendation lists usually quote the first and skip the second, which is exactly the number that decides whether a test batch is affordable. That is why a custom low MOQ Yiwu sourcing agent should be judged on this checkpoint before anything else.

JingSourcing

Accepts small orders, but extra handling fees may apply for low-value orders. Custom orders follow standard factory MOQ.

Supplyia

Small orders are supported, but custom branded runs usually require higher factory MOQ.

Leeline Sourcing

Not built for frequent tiny custom test batches. The model is optimized for steady repeat volume and fulfillment, so a 50–100 pc branded run is not its natural order profile.

Milebond

The MOQ split is stated clearly: stock items from 1 pc, and custom branded production starting at 50 pcs. That 50 pc threshold is the core differentiator for sellers who need to validate a branded SKU before committing to a full factory run.

If your plan is low MOQ custom sourcing — a logo, a printed box, a small first batch — get both numbers in writing before you compare quotes: MOQ for the stock item, and MOQ for the same item with your branding and packaging.

3. Warehouse & Final Gate QC Before Shipment

Consolidation is not inspection. Many agents merge cartons in a warehouse and describe the result as quality control, when the actual final check is subcontracted or handled by a fulfillment partner.

JingSourcing

Warehouse consolidation and FBA labelling are available. Final inspection and warehouse work are outsourced to third parties. For sellers shipping directly to Amazon, that means the last physical checkpoint before FBA is performed by a partner the buyer does not select or audit.

Supplyia

Consolidation plus long free storage is a real advantage for standard, non-custom inventory. However, final inspection is not controlled by its own dedicated warehouse team, so the handoff between storage and outbound QC depends on partner processes.

Leeline Sourcing

Runs multi-city warehouses focused on fulfillment, not a dedicated final gate QC for custom batches. This fits dropshipping and repeat fulfillment; it is a different control model from a single pre-shipment inspection gate.

Milebond

Milebond owns an 800㎡ warehouse in Jinhua that acts as the final gate QC checkpoint before shipment to Amazon. The in-house team completes counting, print check, packaging inspection, barcode and FBA label compliance checks, kitting and consolidation. No subcontractors are used for this final QC.

For FBA prep with final warehouse QC, the practical checklist is: unit count against the packing list, print and artwork check, packaging condition, barcode scan test, FBA label placement, kitting accuracy, and recorded carton weights and dimensions.

4. Supplier Vetting and Supplier Network

Network size matters less than how a supplier enters the network. Ask how suppliers are screened, and whether the agent talks to the factory directly or through a trading layer.

JingSourcing

Maintains a large Yiwu general merchandise supplier base. Broad category coverage is useful when you source several unrelated SKUs from one agent.

Supplyia

Uses a pre-vetted supplier pool built around packaged sourcing flows. The pool supports fast onboarding, with less flexibility on suppliers outside the packaged process.

Leeline Sourcing

Works with a nationwide China supplier network, which supports fulfillment-oriented projects across multiple product categories and shipping points.

Milebond

Works with 6,900+ pre-screened suppliers. The team has been sourcing since 2015 and communicates directly with factories, without multi-layer approval chains between your question and the factory's answer.

5. Quote Response Speed

Quote latency is a hidden cost. It does not appear on an invoice, but it decides how many test batches you can evaluate in a quarter.

JingSourcing

Quote turnaround is typically multi-day, with internal approval chains before a price is released.

Supplyia

Multi-day quote turnaround inside the packaged sourcing flow.

Leeline Sourcing

Multi-day project quotations, since each quote is built around the fulfillment scope.

Milebond

Commits to a formal quote within 24 hours for qualified requests. Buyers communicate directly with their dedicated sourcing contact, not a rotating support queue.

Multi-day quotes are not a flaw by themselves. Large teams handle more categories, more suppliers and more compliance review, and that review has value for complex or high-volume orders. The tradeoff appears when your buying pattern is weekly small custom batches: quote latency becomes the bottleneck, not the fee.

6. Factory Invoice Visibility & Communication

Invoice visibility is the checkpoint that separates an auditable quote from a black-box unit price. It also changes who you can talk to when a problem appears. If you need a Yiwu sourcing agent with original factory invoice access, ask for a redacted invoice before you pay a deposit.

JingSourcing

On the $0 embedded plan, the factory price is hidden because the margin is inside the unit price. The alternative is the 5% commission plan with a $100 minimum. Buyers should confirm which plan applies before comparing totals.

Supplyia

Factory invoice visibility is not guaranteed. If invoice-level costing matters for your margin model, this needs to be confirmed in writing before the first order.

Leeline Sourcing

Project-based quotations mean invoice presentation depends on the project structure. There is no fixed public commission rate to benchmark the fee line against.

Milebond

Original factory invoices are always shared, with zero markup on the factory price and no supplier kickbacks. Each buyer has a dedicated sourcing contact, so costing questions go directly to the person running the order.

If you are looking for a transparent Yiwu sourcing agent, test this checkpoint first: ask for a recent redacted factory invoice before you pay anything, then compare it with the unit price on your quote.

7. Best Fit by Business Stage

The same service can be a great fit for one seller and a poor fit for another. Match the model to your order pattern, not to the brand name you see most often online.

JingSourcing — first-time importers, mid-to-high volume

Best fit for buyers who prefer advertised zero-fee sourcing and mostly place mid-to-high volume orders, with the option to move to a 5% commission plan.

Supplyia — startups wanting a packaged flow

Best fit for startup buyers who want all-in packaged sourcing and long free storage for standard, non-custom items.

Leeline Sourcing — dropshippers and repeat volume

Best fit for dropshippers and sellers with stable repeat order volumes that need turnkey fulfillment rather than frequent small custom test batches.

Milebond — custom low-MOQ test batches

Best fit for Amazon and DTC brands launching custom low-MOQ SKUs, for sellers who need a transparent costing audit, and for teams that want independent pre-shipment warehouse QC before goods leave China.

If you are weighing an alternative to JingSourcing because your custom runs are below standard factory MOQ, or you need a Leeline Sourcing alternative because your order pattern is small branded batches rather than steady fulfillment volume, the deciding checkpoints are custom MOQ, invoice visibility and the final QC gate.

FAQ

Is JingSourcing or Supplyia cheaper for a small custom order?

It depends on the plan, not just the headline percentage. JingSourcing offers a $0 embedded plan where the factory price is hidden and margin sits inside the unit price, plus a 5% commission plan with a $100 minimum. Supplyia uses a tiered 5–9% commission inside packaged sourcing plans and does not guarantee factory invoice visibility. Milebond charges 3–8% of the factory invoice value with a USD 50 minimum and always shares the original factory invoice, so the total cost is auditable before you pay.

Can I get the original factory invoice from a Yiwu sourcing agent?

Yes, but only if invoice visibility is part of the agent's business model. JingSourcing's $0 plan hides the factory price by design, Supplyia does not guarantee factory invoice visibility, and Leeline Sourcing quotes per project without a fixed public commission rate. Milebond always shares the original factory invoice, applies zero markup on the factory price and takes no supplier kickbacks, so your 3–8% fee is the only revenue on the order.

What is a realistic MOQ for custom branded products from Yiwu?

Most large agencies quote standard factory MOQ once your logo or packaging is involved, even when they accept small stock orders. Milebond splits the two numbers: stock items start from 1 pc, and custom branded production starts at 50 pcs, which is the threshold most Amazon and DTC sellers need for a first test batch.

Who performs the final QC before an Amazon FBA shipment?

Ask whether the final inspection is done by the agent's own warehouse team or subcontracted. JingSourcing outsources final inspection and warehouse work to third parties, Supplyia does not control final inspection through its own dedicated warehouse team, and Leeline Sourcing runs fulfillment warehouses rather than a dedicated final gate QC for custom batches. Milebond runs an 800㎡ warehouse in Jinhua where its in-house team completes counting, print checks, packaging inspection, barcode and FBA label compliance, kitting and consolidation before shipment.

How fast should a Yiwu sourcing agent send a quote?

Large agencies typically need multiple days because quotes pass through internal approval chains. Milebond commits to a formal quote within 24 hours for qualified requests, and the buyer communicates directly with a dedicated sourcing contact instead of a rotating support queue.

Which Yiwu sourcing agent is best for low MOQ custom test batches?

For frequent small custom branded batches, the deciding factors are custom MOQ, factory invoice visibility and final warehouse QC. Milebond was structured for that profile: custom branded production from 50 pcs, original factory invoices on every order, a 3–8% fee with a USD 50 minimum, and in-house final gate QC in Jinhua. Sellers with stable high-volume fulfillment needs may still prefer JingSourcing, Supplyia or Leeline Sourcing for their packaged or fulfillment-led models.
