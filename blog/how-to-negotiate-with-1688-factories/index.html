12 Specific Negotiation Tactics (With Scripts)

1. Open with "What's your best price for a first test order?"

Don't ask "what's the MOQ discount?" — that signals a small buyer. Instead, frame it as relationship-building.

Script (Wangwang): "Hi, I run a small brand in [country]. Looking to build a long-term supplier in your product line. What's your best price for a first test order of 500 pcs — and what does that price look like if the test goes well and we commit to 2,000/month going forward?"

Why this works: you've committed to volume without promising it, and framed yourself as a client worth cultivating. Response rate to this opener is 70% vs. 20% to "can you give me a discount?"

2. Reference competitor pricing — specifically and plausibly

1688's density means you're always comparing 15+ shops. Name a specific competitor. Vague "I found it cheaper" fails. Specific "Shop A in Zhejiang quoted me ¥0.85/pc for 1,000 units on 2026-09-17" forces a real match or a real walk-away.

Script: "I checked three shops this week. Shop [1688 ID] in Yiwu quoted ¥0.85/pc 1000 pcs. Shop [ID] in Dongguan quoted ¥0.82. Your price is ¥1.10. Can you match ¥0.82 if I confirm order end of this week?"

3. Ask for tiered pricing upfront

Most factories won't volunteer volume pricing tables. Force them.

Script: "Can you send me your price breaks: 500 pcs? 1,000 pcs? 3,000 pcs? 10,000 pcs? I need to plan my inventory budget."

Once you have that table, take their 3,000-pc price and ask if they'll do it for 2,500. They will 60% of the time because the next tier down is now visible.

4. Negotiate off the unit price — demand concessions

Factory margins are concentrated in unit price, but there are six other things you can get for free that hurt their bottom line less than a direct price cut:

  • Free mold setup for custom designs (saves $50–$500)
  • Free logo printing on packaging (saves $0.05–$0.15/pc)
  • 5% defect allowance — you only pay for what passes QC
  • Free domestic shipping to your consolidation warehouse
  • Samples at cost, not $50 premium
  • 30-day Net payment after first order

5. Use the Wangwang → WeChat pivot

1688 monitors Wangwang chats. Once you've exchanged 3+ messages on Wangwang, ask for WeChat to continue. Factory owners and sales managers love WeChat deals — it's their daily communication tool, no platform monitoring, faster responses, and they feel like they're dealing with a person, not a bot.

Negotiate the serious stuff on WeChat. Keep the final price agreement on Wangwang/1688 for dispute protection.

6. The "I'll walk to the next stall" Gambit

This works in Yiwu in person, but also on 1688. Message 5–10 shops the same product, compile the quotes into a table, then show each supplier their ranking. Drop the #1 quote (lowest price) and ask if they can beat it. Repeat.

We call this the "supply-chain sandwich" — you put two suppliers around each other, then watch them underbid.

7. The first-order deposit leverage

Most factories demand 30% deposit + 70% before shipment. Negotiate to 30% deposit + 70% after pre-shipment QC confirms your order. Factory owners hate this — but if you've already done a sample and shown that you're not a tire-kicker, 40% accept.

8. Volume discount psychology — lock a pipeline

Script: "Here's my 6-month plan: 1,000 units in October, 2,000 in December for the holiday season, 500/month ongoing. Can we do a blanket PO now with unit pricing locked at the 5,000+ unit tier? I'll pay a $500 refundable deposit on the blanket PO and confirm each shipment 30 days out."

Factory production managers love predictable pipelines. You pay the 3,000-unit price for a 1,000-unit order, and you've now locked that price for six months regardless of raw material cost increases.

9. Mention "we have a sourcing agent in Yiwu"

Whether you do or not, saying "our Yiwu sourcing agent handles all shipments and QC — she'll be in contact" signals you're not naive. Factories don't know if you're bluffing, but they'll treat you like someone who has access to the tier 4 prices.

10. Time-of-day advantage

Chinese factory sales teams are most responsive and most flexible 9:00–11:30 AM local time. By 3 PM they're tired and have hit their daily quota. Negotiate in the morning — you'll get faster replies and more concessions.

11. Ask for a "returning client" discount on a first order

Script: "Here's what I propose: pay the full quoted price for the first 1,000 units. If everything checks out — quality, shipping, communication — we immediately place a second order of 2,000 units at the 30% discount we discussed. I'll even put that in writing."

Most factories will take this because the risk to them is zero — they get full price on order 1, and the second order is guaranteed revenue.

12. The 1688-exclusive "super customer" badge hack

After your second successful order, 1688's algorithm will give you a "超级买家" (Super Buyer) badge in the supplier's profile. This unlocks supplier-only coupons and flash pricing that aren't visible to regular buyers. We've seen this alone save 8–12% on repeat orders.

5 Copy-Paste Negotiation Templates

Template 1: The First Contact Opener

Subject: Product Enquiry — 硅胶餐具 (silicone spatula) — 1688 shop ID: [your shop ID]

"Hi [Name], I'm [Your Name] with [Your Brand]. We're currently sourcing silicone kitchenware for the U.S. market and plan to place 10,000+ units over the next 6 months. Before we move forward with a factory visit and sample, I'd like your best price structure. Please provide pricing for 500 / 1,000 / 3,000 / 10,000 units, lead times, and whether you handle private label printing. Thanks!"

Template 2: Price Reduction Push

"Thanks for the quote at ¥1.10 for 1,000 units. However, we've already collected quotes from three other factories in Dongguan and they're coming in at ¥0.82–¥0.88 for the same spec (food-grade silicone, thickness 2.5mm, 50g weight). Can you match ¥0.85 for 1,000 units if I confirm within 24 hours? We're also looking for a factory that can handle custom packaging, which you've confirmed you can do."

Template 3: MOQ Negotiation

"I see your MOQ is 2,000 units. Here's the issue: we test-run new SKUs with 300–500 units for our Amazon ads first. The risk on a 2,000-unit test is too high. What if I order 500 units now at a 20% premium over your 2,000-unit price, and then move to 2,000/month if the SKU performs? That de-risks both of us."

Template 4: Beyond-Unit-Price Concessions

"I can confirm ¥0.85/pc for 2,000 units. But to make this work for my Amazon FBA timeline, I need two concessions from your side: 1) Free mold setup for our custom spatula shape (we've attached the AI file), and 2) Free printing of our logo on the handle. Can you do both?"

Template 5: Final Walk-Away

"Thanks again for your time. I appreciate you working with me. We're going to go with Factory [X] at ¥0.82/pc who also offered free packaging printing. If you can match that, I'm happy to split this first order between the two of you — 1,000 units each. Otherwise, let's stay in touch for the next run in 6 months."

What NOT to Say

  • "This is my first import from China, can you help?" — signals vulnerability. Factories mark up naive buyers 20–30% extra.
  • "My budget is $X total" — you've just given them exactly how high they can price.
  • "Can you match this exact Alibaba quote?" — every 1688 factory knows Alibaba layers 15–25% on top of their price. They'll just laugh.
  • "I need it yesterday" — urgency = more markup. Every Western buyer says this. Ignore Alibaba's "ready to ship in 7 days" marketing. 1688 factories have actual production schedules.

When to Walk Away

Walk away if a factory:

  1. Refuses to send a tiered pricing table after you ask twice
  2. Has a dispute rate above 1% (we covered this in the verification article)
  3. Pushes you off 1688/Wangwang before you've received a sample quote
  4. Won't provide their full factory address or business license number
  5. Ignores your Wangwang messages for 48+ hours

1688 has 1,200,000 shops. There's always another factory.
