1688 Agent Pre-Order Checklist: 5 Questions That Filter 90% of Black-Box Agents

Before you wire any deposit. Before you share your product list. Ask these 5 questions. A transparent agent will answer all five in one paragraph. A black-box middleman will give you three excuses, one pivot, and a "why don't we just start."

Get Free Vetting Help
9 min read · Updated Sep 2026

This is not theoretical.

We've sat with buyers who paid 15–30% hidden markup on 1688 orders and only found out six months later. We've seen agents keep two sets of invoices — one for you, one for themselves — where the gap disappears into a personal Alipay account.

There is a reliable pattern in how these conversations go.

Our long guide to buying from 1688 without an agent covers the whole model. This checklist is the shortcut. Five questions. Asked in five minutes. Zero money at risk.

Why These 5 Questions Work

Every black-box agent uses the same playbook: quote you a small explicit fee (2–5%), hide the real markup in inflated 1688 prices and padded shipping, then obfuscate the trail so you can't verify. These five questions attack that playbook from five angles — price source, negotiation proof, shipping transparency, fee structure, and exit clause. If any one of them gets a fuzzy answer, walk away.

Transparent agents have nothing to hide, so they answer crisply. Black-box agents can't afford to answer — so they deflect.

Question 1: The 1688 Listing Link

What you're really asking: "Is the price you're quoting me the real 1688 price, or have you marked it up before I even see it?"

A transparent agent treats 1688 as your open marketplace — they find, verify, negotiate, and pass the link to you. The screenshot proves the supplier's original list price and shows you what was negotiated. A black-box agent almost never shares the link, because the gap between the 1688 CNY price and the USD they quoted you is where their hidden margin lives.

Red flag: "We aggregate from many suppliers, links aren't meaningful." — that's code for "I won't show you the source because it exposes the markup."

Question 2: The Negotiation Log

What you're really asking: "Did you actually negotiate anything, or did you just add your fee on top of the supplier's first price and call it a discount?"

Serious sourcing teams use DingTalk or WeCom for supplier communication, and they archive every thread. Ask to see one from a similar product. You should see specific numbers: supplier asked ¥45, agent offered ¥38, they settled at ¥40 with free cartons. If you see generic chat ("hello interested in your product") and no price push, they're not negotiating for you — they're fulfilling.

Question 3: Shipping Invoices and Forwarder Choice

What you're really asking: "Will the shipping number on my invoice match the shipping number the forwarder charged, or are you adding freight markup on top?"

Shipping is where 60% of hidden markups hide. A black-box agent quotes you $1,200 for sea freight, pays the forwarder $750, and pockets $450. The clean test: does the forwarder bill you directly? If yes, you compare their number to any quote you get from Flexport or a freight forwarder directory. If the agent insists on handling everything and won't let you talk to the forwarder — walk.

Question 4: Fee Structure and No-Kickback Clause

What you're really asking: "Is there any other money flowing to this agent that I'm not seeing?"

The industry term is "rebate" — the factory pays the agent 5–15% to send you their way, and you never know. Transparent agents explicitly disclaim kickbacks in their contract. Black-box agents will frame their low fee as a loss leader: "we only charge 2% because the suppliers give us volume discounts." That discount is coming out of your negotiation power, not their margin.

Question 5: Audit and Refund Policy

What you're really asking: "What happens when I find out you overcharged me?"

A transparent agent has nothing to fear from an audit, so they'll offer a price-match guarantee or even a penalty for hidden markup (2× the difference is a strong signal). A black-box agent will dodge this with complexity — "our contract is standard," "this slows things down," "other clients don't ask for this."

Email Template to Send Before Hiring

Copy-paste this. Send it before any call. Tell them you need written answers.

Subject: Pre-Order Transparency Questions — [Your Brand]

Hi [Agent Name],

Thanks for the proposal. Before we move forward, I need written answers to five questions. This is standard for our supplier due diligence — it protects both of us.

  1. When you quote a product, will you share the exact 1688 listing URL and supplier screenshot?
  2. Can you show a negotiation log example for a similar product (chat screenshots with supplier)?
  3. Will the forwarder invoice come to me directly, and may I choose my own forwarder?
  4. List every fee you charge, with no kickbacks or hidden markup. Will you sign a no-rebate clause?

I'd like your responses in writing by EOD tomorrow so we can schedule the kickoff call.

Best,
[Your Name]

How to Use This in Your First Call

  1. Send the email above before the call. Say you'll go over answers together.
  2. Open with question 1. Watch for defensiveness. "Can you pull up an example 1688 link from last week?" A good agent does it on the call in 20 seconds. A bad one says "I'll get that after."
  3. Don't let them pivot. Black-box agents will switch to "our service is fast / we have 10 years / we know the best factories." That's a distraction. Say: "Great, I'll take that as yes you can answer. Can we go back to the negotiation log?"
  4. Score them. 5/5 = proceed. 3–4 = red lines, demand fixes. 0–2 = move on.
