1688 Payment Methods for Foreign Buyers: Complete 2026 Comparison

You've registered, you've negotiated a great price, you've verified the factory. Now the final hurdle: how do you actually pay a Chinese company from abroad without wiring $5,000 into the void? We've used every method on this list.

Read the Full 1688 Guide
9 min read · Updated Sep 2026

This is the point where most first-time 1688 buyers freeze. The product page has a green "Buy Now" button, but clicking it throws up payment methods they've never heard of.

Alipay with a Chinese bank account? That's for locals. UnionPay? Your card doesn't have it. WeChat Pay? Same problem. And wiring $3,500 via SWIFT to a factory in Zhejiang feels like sending cash in the mail.

There are six legitimate payment channels for foreign buyers on 1688. We'll walk through each one with real fee numbers, speed, risk level, and when to use it.

The Payment Wall Explained

1688 is built for domestic Chinese trade. Every payment route you see on the platform requires the seller to have a Chinese Alipay or corporate bank account — and most won't enable international cross-border pay (KJB) unless you ask.

Before you can pay anything, three things must be true:

  1. Your 1688 account has KJB enabled (see our registration article if you skipped this)
  2. The supplier's shop has the green 跨境专供 (Cross-Border Ready) badge
  3. You're logged in on the 1688 website, not a mirror site or agent reskin

If any one is missing, your payment will fail with a generic error and you'll think 1688 doesn't serve foreigners.

KJB (Cross-Border Pay): 1688's Official Channel — Deep Dive

跨境宝 (Kuajing Bao / KJB) is Alibaba's official cross-border payment channel. Think of it as a wrapper around Alipay that converts your foreign currency to RMB and handles Chinese regulatory reporting. This is the default payment method you should use unless KJB rejects you.

How It Works

Once KJB is enabled on your account, you'll see three payment buttons at checkout: Visa/Mastercard, WorldFirst, and Zyla. Each has different fees and limits.

Visa/Mastercard Through KJB

  • Fees: Flat 3.5% — the only transparent fee. No hidden FX markup.
  • Speed: Instant. Payment confirms in 1–2 minutes.
  • Limits: $50,000/day, $200,000/year personal account.
  • Escrow protection: Yes. 1688 holds funds until you confirm receipt. You get a full refund if the product doesn't match.
  • Best for: Sample orders, orders under $3,000, importers from any country.

WorldFirst Through KJB

WorldFirst was acquired by Ant Financial (Alibaba's parent) in 2019 and now has direct API integration with 1688. If you plan to order $3,000+/month, WorldFirst is the cheapest option.

  • Fees: 1.0–2.0% depending on monthly volume. No FX markup.
  • Speed: 1–3 business days (you load WorldFirst via bank transfer first)
  • Limits: $200,000/day business account, $50,000/day personal
  • Escrow protection: Yes, same as credit cards through KJB
  • Availability: US, UK, EU (all 27), AU, SG, CA, HK, and 20+ more

WorldFirst's personal account minimums are very low — you can open one with no deposit. The 1.0% rate kicks in at ~$20,000/month volume.

Zyla Through KJB

Zyla is the new 2026 entrant — a Polish-based fintech that acts as a payment facilitator between 1688 and 150+ countries where WorldFirst and traditional cards struggle. Specifically useful for importers in Africa, the Middle East, and Latin America.

  • Fees: Flat 1.25%
  • Speed: Instant (card) or 1–2 days (bank transfer)
  • Limits: $100,000/day
  • Escrow protection: Yes
  • Availability: 150+ countries including Nigeria, Kenya, Egypt, Mexico, Brazil, Argentina

Alternative Payment Channels (When KJB Doesn't Work)

1. Local Payment Agent (the most common fallback)

If you're in a country not supported by KJB (rare but some nations fall through the cracks), or if KJB rejected your ID verification, a local payment agent handles it. These are registered trading companies in Yiwu who accept your local currency via Wise/Payoneer/Western Union and pay 1688 in RMB from their own Alipay account.

  • Fees: 2–4% on top of interbank FX. Milebond charges a flat 2.5% with no markup.
  • Speed: Same day if you send money in the morning; next day otherwise
  • Risk: Medium — you're trusting a third party to pass through the money. But 1688's escrow still protects the product side.
  • Red flag: Any agent asking you to send money to a personal bank account. Always send to a company account.

2. Fee-Only Buying Services

Similar to payment agents but usually bundled with sourcing and QC. You don't get direct 1688 access — the service browses, purchases, and handles everything for you. Fees are typically 3–5% plus payment costs.

3. Alipay With a Chinese Bank Account (not realistic for most)

Alipay with a Chinese UnionPay card has zero fees, instant speed, and full escrow. But you'd need to open a personal RMB account in mainland China — which requires a Chinese phone number, address, and in-person bank visit. Not worth it unless you're living in China long-term.

4. SWIFT Bank Transfer (direct to factory)

The old-fashioned way. Transfer USD/EUR directly to the factory's corporate account via your bank's international wire.

  • Fees: $30–$50 outgoing bank fee + interbank FX spread (1–3%) + sometimes a $15 Chinese bank receiving fee
  • Speed: 3–5 business days
  • Escrow protection: NONE. The factory has the money immediately. If they don't ship, 1688 won't help — the payment was off-platform.
  • When to use: Established suppliers with 5+ orders, $10,000+ bulk payments, and a mutual trust level. We do this maybe 10% of the time.

Escrow Protection — The Non-Negotiable

Let's be absolutely clear on this. Any payment method not routed through 1688's official escrow (支付宝担保交易) leaves you with zero protection.

Here's how 1688 escrow works:

  1. You pay — the money goes to an Alibaba-owned holding account, not the factory.
  2. The factory ships, provides tracking.
  3. You receive the goods, confirm on 1688 that everything matches.
  4. Money is released to the factory.

If you open a dispute within 15 days, 1688's mediation team reviews evidence from both sides. 1688 refunds ~85% of disputes favoring buyers who provide clear photos of defects. This protection disappears if you wire directly via SWIFT or pay personal Alipay/WeChat.

Rule: Only pay directly via SWIFT after you've placed 3+ successful orders with the same factory. The ~1% fee savings aren't worth being one bad shipment away from zero recourse.

3 Payment Scams to Avoid

1. "Pay to my personal Alipay / WeChat"

A factory that asks for payment to a personal account (Alipay: 小明, WeChat: xiaoming_88) is either a trading company hiding margins or planning to disappear once you've sent money. Never do this.

2. Fake 1688 mirror sites

URLs like 1688pay.com or 1688-order.net will mimic the checkout page and steal your card details. Always confirm the URL bar says 1688.com/order/confirm.htm or ends with .aliyun.com (Alibaba's cloud infrastructure).

3. The "WeChat red packet" advance scam

A factory asks you to send a small "good faith deposit" via WeChat red packet (max ~$150) before processing your order. They take the money and block you. Never send advance payments outside 1688's checkout flow.

Conclusion

For 95% of foreign 1688 buyers, the answer is simple: enable KJB, use WorldFirst for orders over $3,000, use a credit card for samples under $3,000, and never use SWIFT direct until you've established trust.

The only legitimate reason to use a local payment agent is if you're from a country KJB doesn't support — and even then, you should be routing through someone who has a proper company account, not a friend with a WeChat QR code.
