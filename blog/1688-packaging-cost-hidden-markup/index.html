1688 Packaging Cost Hidden Markup: Why You Pay 60% More Than the Factory Does

Packaging is the most aggressively marked-up line item in sourcing. We break down why, show benchmark factory prices by box type and quantity, and give you the 4 scam patterns to spot before you pay.

Get Free Packaging Audit
7 min read · Updated Sep 2026

Product price? You check 1688. Shipping? You Google DHL. But packaging? Most importers skip it entirely.

That's why packaging gets marked up harder than any other line item — 40–60% is standard, and we've seen 100%+ on small orders. Agents know packaging is opaque, hard to price-compare, and looks like a "necessary cost" in the invoice. A $0.05 factory box becomes $0.08 from the agent, which becomes $0.12 you pay — no one questions the number.

In this article, we give you 2026 market benchmark prices for every packaging type, break down four packaging scam patterns, and show you exactly how to source packaging yourself on 1688 — cheaper than what 90% of agents quote.

Why Packaging Gets Marked Up So Aggressively

Three structural reasons:

  • Packaging is a % of total order value. A $0.05 box × 1,000 units = $50 product cost for packaging. Mark up to $0.08 → $80. But if the product is $10/unit ($10,000 order), that $80 packaging disappears into the noise — and the agent's $30 markup is invisible.
  • Cross-supplier consolidation is hidden. Most agents don't buy packaging from the factory making the product. They buy it from a nearby Yiwu packaging shop at discount, then mark it up. No one connects these two suppliers in the invoice.
  • "Mandatory upgrade" myth. Agents tell clients, "We had to upgrade to a better box because the factory's original was too flimsy" — implying they did you a favor, while charging 30% more than the actual cost of the upgrade.

4 Red Flags to Spot Packaging Markup

1. "We Specialized Your Packaging" Without Details

Agents love to throw the phrase "we upgraded your packaging for better protection" or "we designed a custom box" — without providing photos of the original vs. the "upgrade." The difference between a $0.32 standard box and a $0.52 printed box is $0.20 — but agents charge an extra $0.30 and pocket the $0.10 without telling you.

What to ask: "Can you send a photo of the original factory box and the 'upgraded' box side-by-side, plus the packaging supplier's invoice?"

2. Packaging Cost Is More Than 15–20% of Product Cost

For most consumer goods, packaging should be 5–15% of the product unit cost. If your $3/unit product has packaging costing $0.60/unit — that's 20% — and red flags the agent is padding the packaging number.

Exception: luxury gift boxes or specialized protective packaging (hard cases, foam inserts) can go to 25–30% — but these are the minority.

3. One Packaging Quote Covers Everything (No Breakdown)

A legitimate packaging supplier invoice breaks down: box + printing + label + assembly + shipping to factory. Agents quote one bundled "packaging fee" with zero detail. This makes it impossible to cross-check against the benchmark table above.

4. "This Box Is Mandatory — The Factory Refuses Your Original"

This is a bluff 80% of the time. We've never seen a factory refuse a client's box specification if they're paying for it. What's happening: the agent already bought a batch of boxes they're trying to offload to you at a markup, and they don't want to waste that inventory.

How to Self-Source Packaging on 1688

This takes about 15 minutes and can save you 40–60% on packaging vs. agent quotes.

  1. Log into 1688.com with your account (Alibaba account works too)
  2. Search for your box type in English or Chinese: try "custom printed corrugated box" or in Chinese "定制彩色瓦楞纸盒"
  3. Filter by location to Yiwu, Wenzhou, or Dongguan — these are China's main packaging clusters, and suppliers there are the cheapest
  4. Open 3–4 supplier listings and check their tier prices at your quantity (most show these automatically on the product page)
  5. Use Wangwang (1688 chat) to ask the exact spec: "For 2,000 pieces, 4-color print, 3-ply B-flute, 25×18×15cm — what's your EXW Yiwu price?"
  6. Compare 3 quotes — the lowest one is usually your best bet. Yiwu packaging is extremely price-competitive.
  7. Have the packaging supplier deliver direct to your product factory — both are often within 30–60 km in Zhejiang province; delivery is cheap or free.
Pro tip: 1688 packaging suppliers almost always have free sample programs. Ask for 2–3 physical samples to compare print quality and box thickness before committing.

FAQ

Can packaging suppliers on 1688 handle Amazon FBA prep?
Yes — most Yiwu and Dongguan packaging suppliers offer FBA label printing, polybagging, and carton configuration as optional add-ons. Ask: "Do you do Amazon FBA FNSKU labels and carton configuration?" They'll quote that in minutes. Usually $0.03–$0.05 per unit for prep work.
Is it cheaper to have the product factory also do the packaging, or to source it separately?
For simple packaging (brown boxes, plain polybags) the factory is usually fine and often cheaper because it's included in their setup. For custom color boxes, printed bags, or rigid gift boxes — always source separately from a dedicated packaging supplier. Factories buy packaging from 1688 packaging companies themselves and mark it up 20–40%. Going direct cuts that out.
My agent said they'll "handle the whole packaging coordination" — should I pay extra for that?
No. "Coordinating" packaging means sending an email from the 1688 packaging supplier to your product factory. That's a 2-minute task. Don't pay an agent a percentage fee for it. A flat $20–$50 coordination fee is fair, but any percentage fee is markup.
What's the minimum order for custom printed packaging on 1688?
Most printed box suppliers on 1688 have a 500-box MOQ for single-color and 1,000-box MOQ for full-color CMYK. Some small Yiwu shops will do 300 boxes with a small setup fee. Rigid gift boxes typically start at 200 MOQ.
