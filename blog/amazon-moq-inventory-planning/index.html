Amazon MOQ & Inventory Planning

What Is the Right MOQ for Amazon Sellers? A Practical Guide to Sourcing from China

A low MOQ is not automatically a good purchasing decision. How to balance demand, production lead time, shipping and inventory risk when sourcing from China.

When sourcing products from China for Amazon, one of the most important decisions is how much to order.

A low MOQ may seem attractive because it reduces the initial investment. However, ordering too little can create replenishment pressure, especially when production and international shipping take time.

The right MOQ is not simply the smallest quantity a supplier accepts. It is the quantity that balances product demand, inventory investment, production lead time and replenishment risk.

In this guide, we explain how Amazon sellers can evaluate MOQ, understand factory requirements and make more informed purchasing decisions when sourcing from China.

What Does MOQ Mean in China Sourcing?

MOQ stands for Minimum Order Quantity. It is the minimum quantity a supplier or manufacturer is willing to accept for an order.

MOQ requirements vary depending on product type, materials, customization, production processes and factory policies.

For example, products already available in supplier stock may be purchased in smaller quantities. Products requiring new production may have higher MOQ requirements because factories need to arrange materials, labor, production lines and packaging.

Can You Source Products from China with an MOQ of 1 Piece?

Yes, in some cases.

When a supplier has available stock, it may be possible to source from 1 piece. This can be useful for samples, product evaluation or small purchasing needs.

However, in-stock availability should not be confused with made-to-order production.

If a product requires new manufacturing, customization or special packaging, the factory may require a higher MOQ.

At Milebond, in-stock sourcing can start from 1 piece. For made-to-order products, the typical MOQ starts at around 100 pieces, depending on the product, specifications and factory requirements.

What Is a Typical MOQ for Products Made in China?

There is no single MOQ that applies to every product.

The actual minimum quantity depends on:

  • Product category.
  • Raw material requirements.
  • Product dimensions and specifications.
  • Customization requirements.
  • Packaging requirements.
  • Production process.
  • Factory capacity and policies.

For Milebond sourcing projects, the typical made-to-order MOQ starts at around 100 pieces. This is a general reference, not a universal guarantee.

Why the Lowest MOQ Is Not Always the Best Choice

A low MOQ can reduce the amount of money tied up in initial inventory. But it does not automatically create the best purchasing decision.

If your products sell faster than expected, a small order may run out before the next shipment arrives.

This is particularly important for Amazon sellers because replenishment involves more than factory production. It also involves inspection, shipment preparation, international transportation and receiving inventory at the destination.

A low MOQ can create three practical risks:

  1. Stockout risk. Inventory may run out before the next batch is available.
  2. Replenishment pressure. More frequent orders mean less buffer for production or shipping delays.
  3. Higher operational costs from frequent small orders. Repeating sourcing, inspection and shipment coordination adds work per unit.

On the other hand, ordering too much can increase storage costs, tie up working capital and create excess inventory risk.

The goal is not to order as little as possible or as much as possible. The goal is to find a quantity that matches realistic demand and replenishment timing.

How Production Lead Time Affects Your MOQ

Production lead time directly affects how much inventory an Amazon seller may need to keep available.

For many sourcing projects, production takes several weeks. At Milebond, typical production lead time is around 25–30 days, depending on the product and factory.

Shipping time is additional.

This means sellers should not calculate their purchasing quantity based only on the number of units they expect to sell in the next few days.

They should also consider how long it will take to produce and replenish the next batch.

How to Think About Your First Purchase Quantity

A simple way to frame the decision is:

Estimated Inventory Needed = Expected Daily Sales × Total Replenishment Lead Time + Safety Stock
  • Expected Daily Sales: the units you realistically expect to sell per day.
  • Total Replenishment Lead Time: the total time from purchasing decision and production to goods being available to sell.
  • Safety Stock: a buffer for demand changes and transport delays.

This is a simplified planning framework, not a universal purchasing formula. Actual purchasing decisions should also consider current inventory, sales fluctuations, cash flow, seasonality and supplier reliability.

Example:

If a product sells an estimated 10 units per day and the total replenishment lead time is 45 days, the seller may need to plan for approximately 450 units of demand during that period, before adding an appropriate safety stock allowance.

This does not mean every seller should order 450 units. Existing inventory, incoming shipments, demand changes and budget constraints must also be considered.

Air Freight vs. Sea Freight: How Shipping Changes Your Inventory Plan

Air freight may help with urgent replenishment because it is generally faster than sea freight. However, it usually comes with higher transportation costs.

Sea freight is often considered for larger shipments when delivery schedules allow.

Amazon sellers should evaluate transportation cost, shipment size, urgency and inventory risk together.

Air freight can be a solution for selected urgent shipments, but it should not automatically become the default replenishment strategy.

How to Manage MOQ Across Multiple Products and Suppliers

Amazon brands often sell multiple products sourced from different factories.

Each supplier may have different MOQ requirements, production schedules and packaging standards.

Managing multiple factories independently can make purchasing and shipment planning more complicated.

A China sourcing partner can help coordinate supplier communication, production follow-up, product inspection, goods receiving and shipment preparation.

When timing and logistics requirements allow, products from different suppliers may also be consolidated.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a lower MOQ always cheaper?

Not necessarily. A lower MOQ reduces the initial cash outlay, but frequent small orders can increase per-unit handling, inspection and shipping coordination. The lowest total cost depends on sales velocity, lead time and how much buffer stock you need.

Can Milebond source from 1 piece?

For products available in supplier stock, sourcing can start from 1 piece. Made-to-order products generally start at around 100 pieces, depending on the product and factory.

How long does production take before shipping?

Typical production lead time is around 25–30 days, depending on the product and factory. Shipping time must be planned separately.

Can I combine products from different factories?

Consolidation may be possible when production schedules, product characteristics, packaging requirements and shipping arrangements allow. It is evaluated per order.
