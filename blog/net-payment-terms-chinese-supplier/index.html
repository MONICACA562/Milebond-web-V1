How to Get Net 30 / Net 60 from Chinese Suppliers — Even as a Small Buyer

Net 60 terms beat a 5% price cut for cash flow. Here's the step-by-step method to get Net 30/60, even with small orders.

Get Free Consultation
11 min read · Updated Sep 2026

You place an order for $8,000. You pay 30% ($2,400) when you confirm the order. Forty-five days later, the goods are ready, and you pay the remaining 70% ($5,600) before shipment. Then the goods spend 30 days on the ocean. Then they spend another 15 days in FBA before they start selling.

Let's count the days: from the moment you pay the deposit to the moment you receive your first dollar from a customer, approximately 90 days pass. During those 90 days, your $8,000 is doing absolutely nothing for you. It's not funding new products. It's not running PPC ads. It's not covering operating expenses. It's sitting in a Chinese factory's bank account and then on a boat.

Now imagine if, instead of paying 30/70 before shipment, you paid Net 45 — meaning you pay the full amount 45 days after the goods ship. Your money leaves your account 45 days later. The goods arrive at FBA around the same time your payment is due. You're essentially selling the product before you pay for it.

That 45-day difference on an $8,000 order, at a 15% annual cost of capital (what most small sellers pay on business credit cards or lines of credit), is worth approximately $150 per order in financing cost savings. On 6 orders a year, that's $900. But the real value isn't the $900 — it's the $8,000 of working capital you get to keep in your business for an extra 45 days on every order.

For a small seller with $30,000 in total working capital, that's the difference between funding 3 product lines and 4. Between running ads at full budget and pausing campaigns when cash gets tight. Between growing and treading water.

Net 30/60 payment terms are worth more than a 5% price cut. And almost no small buyer knows how to get them.

Why Suppliers Won't Give You Net Terms (And What Changes Their Mind)

Let's be honest about why Chinese suppliers insist on 30/70 upfront payment for new buyers:

  1. They don't know you. You're a name on an email from another country. If they ship $8,000 of goods on Net 30 and you disappear, they're out $8,000 with no practical way to collect.
  2. They've been burned before. Every supplier has a story about a foreign buyer who received goods on credit and then vanished, or claimed "quality issues" to avoid paying, or went bankrupt. The 30/70 structure is their defense mechanism.
  3. Their own cash flow is tight. Most small Chinese factories operate on thin margins and need the 30% deposit to buy raw materials. They can't afford to finance your order for 30-60 days.

These are real barriers. But they're not permanent. Here's what changes a supplier's mind:

  • A track record of on-time payments. After 3-5 smooth orders, the supplier knows you pay. The risk of non-payment drops dramatically.
  • Order volume and consistency. If you're ordering every month like clockwork, you're a reliable revenue stream — worth extending credit to keep.
  • A credit-worthy intermediary. If a reputable local agent with a long-standing relationship with the supplier vouches for you (or even takes on the payment risk), the supplier will extend Net terms from day one.
  • Trade credit insurance. If you can offer credit insurance (via companies like Coface, Atradius, or China's Sinosure), the supplier's non-payment risk is transferred to the insurer — and they'll extend Net terms.

The first two take time. The last two can get you Net terms from your very first order — if you know how to structure them.

Real Case: 30/70 Upfront → Net 45 on Order Three

The client: an FBA seller doing kitchen goods, $5,000–$10,000 per order, 4-6 orders per year.

The starting point: new supplier, standard 30% deposit / 70% before shipment. The client was frustrated because every order tied up cash for 60+ days before they could sell the product.

What we did:

Order 1 (30/70): Placed the first order at standard terms. We managed the entire process — supplier communication, production follow-up, pre-shipment inspection, logistics. The order went smoothly: on-time delivery, quality passed inspection, no issues. The client paid the balance on the day it was due.

Order 2 (30/70, but with a conversation): Before placing the second order, we had a call with the supplier's sales manager. We said: "Our client is very happy with the first order. They plan to order every 6-8 weeks. For the third order, would you be open to discussing Net 15 terms? We can provide bank references and a credit application." The supplier said they'd consider it after the second order went smoothly.

Order 2 went smoothly. On-time, quality passed, client paid on time.

Order 3 (Net 15): We submitted a simple credit application: company registration, bank reference, 6 months of bank statements showing consistent cash flow, and two trade references from other Chinese suppliers. The supplier agreed to Net 15 from B/L date (bill of lading date — meaning payment is due 15 days after the ship departs).

Order 5 (Net 30): After two more smooth Net 15 orders, we renegotiated to Net 30 from B/L date.

Order 8 (Net 45): After three more Net 30 orders with consistent volume, we renegotiated to Net 45 from B/L date.

The result: From order 8 onward, the client's cash flow improved dramatically:

  • Before: $8,000 paid 100% before shipment, cash tied up ~75 days (production + ocean + FBA inbound)
  • After: $8,000 paid 45 days after B/L, cash tied up ~30 days (ocean + FBA inbound, and payment is due around the time goods arrive at FBA)
  • Cash flow improvement: ~45 days of float on every order = ~$4,000 average cash freed per order

The client used that freed cash to launch two additional SKUs in the following year.

The 4-Step Method to Get Net Payment Terms

Step 1: Build a Perfect Payment Track Record (Orders 1-3)

You cannot negotiate Net terms until the supplier trusts you. The fastest way to build trust is a perfect payment track record:

  • Pay the deposit within 24 hours of PO confirmation. Don't make them chase you.
  • Pay the balance within 24 hours of receiving the inspection report and bill of lading copy. Again, speed matters.
  • Never dispute a payment over a minor issue. If there's a small quality problem (2% defects instead of 1%), handle it as a discount on the next order, not by withholding payment on the current order. Withholding payment destroys trust faster than anything.
  • Communicate proactively. If there's going to be a delay in payment (bank holiday, wire issue), tell the supplier before the due date. Don't make them wonder where the money is.

After 3 perfect orders, you have the credibility to ask for Net terms. Before that, don't even try — you'll get a flat no and signal that you're a difficult buyer.

Step 2: Submit a Professional Credit Application

When you ask for Net terms, don't just say "can we get Net 30?" Present it as a formal credit application. This signals that you're a serious business, not a casual buyer.

Your credit application should include:

  • Company registration documents (business license, certificate of incorporation)
  • Bank reference letter from your business bank (ask your bank for a "standard trade reference letter" — they know what this is)
  • 6 months of business bank statements (redact sensitive info if needed, but show consistent cash flow and sufficient balances)
  • 2-3 trade references from other Chinese suppliers you've paid on time (ask their permission first)
  • A simple credit request form: company name, contact, annual purchase volume with this supplier, requested terms (Net 15 / Net 30), and a signature

This package takes an afternoon to assemble. It increases your chance of getting Net terms from "maybe" to "probably" because it reduces the supplier's perceived risk — they can see you're a real business with real cash flow and a history of paying other suppliers.

Step 3: Start Small and Escalate

Don't ask for Net 60 on your first request. That's too big a jump and you'll get a no. Use this ladder:

StageTermsWhen to Ask
Starting30% deposit / 70% before shipmentOrder 1
First askNet 15 from B/L dateAfter 3 perfect orders
Second askNet 30 from B/L dateAfter 3 perfect Net 15 orders
Third askNet 45 from B/L dateAfter 3 perfect Net 30 orders + growing volume
FinalNet 60 from B/L dateOnly for high-volume, long-term relationships (1+ year, $50K+/year)

Each step is a small, reasonable ask that the supplier can say yes to without taking on excessive risk. Trying to jump from 30/70 to Net 60 in one step is like asking for a credit card with a $50,000 limit when you have no credit history — the answer is no, and you've signaled you don't understand how credit works.

Key negotiation point: "from B/L date" (bill of lading date) is much better for you than "from invoice date" or "from delivery date." B/L date is when the ship departs — the goods are already on the way, and the clock starts ticking. Always specify Net X from B/L date in writing.

Step 4: Use an Agent's Credit to Get There Faster (or From Day One)

If you don't want to wait 9-12 months to climb the Net terms ladder, there's a faster way: work with a local agent who has an established credit relationship with suppliers.

Here's how it works:

  • The agent has been working with the supplier for years. The supplier knows the agent pays on time, every time.
  • When you place an order through the agent, the supplier extends Net terms to the agent (because they trust the agent), and the agent passes those terms through to you.
  • Effectively, you get Net 30/45 from your very first order — no 3-order waiting period, no credit application, no bank references.

This is one of the most underrated values of working with a reputable local agent. Most buyers think agents are just for "finding suppliers" and "negotiating prices." But the credit access can be worth far more than any price negotiation — because it directly improves your cash flow on every single order.

Important caveat: make sure the agent is transparent about how this works. Some agents use their credit relationship as a reason to charge you a higher "financing fee" or mark up the unit price. A reputable agent charges a transparent service fee and passes through the Net terms at no extra cost — because the Net terms are a benefit of the agent's relationship, not a separate service they're selling you.

What Net Terms Are Actually Worth (The Math)

Let's do the math on why Net terms matter more than price for a small seller.

Scenario A (30/70 upfront):

  • Order value: $8,000
  • Payment: $2,400 at order (day 0), $5,600 before shipment (day 35)
  • Goods arrive at FBA: day 65
  • First sale: day 80
  • Cash tied up: $8,000 for ~80 days average
  • At 15% annual cost of capital: $8,000 × 15% × (80/365) = $263 per order in financing cost

Scenario B (Net 45 from B/L):

  • Order value: $8,000
  • Payment: $0 at order, $8,000 due 45 days after B/L (B/L = day 35, so payment due day 80)
  • Goods arrive at FBA: day 65
  • First sale: day 80 (same day payment is due — you're selling as you pay)
  • Cash tied up: $0 for the first 80 days (you pay from sales revenue)
  • Financing cost: ~$0 (you're using supplier credit, not your own capital)

Difference: $263 per order in direct financing cost savings. On 6 orders/year = $1,578/year.

But the indirect value is much larger. In Scenario B, you have $8,000 of your own capital free during those 80 days. You can use it to:

  • Place a second order with a different supplier (doubling your product velocity)
  • Run $8,000 of PPC ads (at a 3:1 ROAS, that's $24,000 in revenue)
  • Cover 2-3 months of operating expenses without dipping into personal savings
  • Act as a buffer against a slow-selling product

For a small seller with $30,000 in working capital, Net 45 terms on one $8,000 product line frees up 27% of your total capital. That's not a minor optimization. That's a structural change in what your business can afford to do.

The Bottom Line

Payment terms are the most under-negotiated lever in sourcing. Every buyer negotiates price. Almost no buyer systematically negotiates payment terms. But for a small seller, the cash flow benefit of Net 30/45 terms is often worth more than a 5% price reduction — because price affects margin, but payment terms affect how much capital you have to grow.

The 4-step method (build a perfect track record, submit a professional credit application, escalate gradually from Net 15 to Net 45, and use an agent's credit to accelerate) will get you Net terms with most suppliers within 6-12 months. With a reputable local agent, you can get there from day one.
