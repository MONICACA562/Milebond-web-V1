Let's talk about the line item that 9 out of 10 importers skip auditing: freight.

Your agent quotes $1,800 "all-in shipping" from Ningbo to Los Angeles for a 30 kg parcel. It feels reasonable. But if you independently check DHL Express retail rates for the same weight, route, and service level — you get $1,240. If you find the actual forwarder invoice? $800.

That $1,000 gap is not a surprise. Independent agents commonly layer a 50–120% markup on logistics, and many pocket the difference without disclosure. Why? Because importers trust the agent's "combined quote" for product + shipping as a single number — and never dig into the split.

This article gives you the complete audit toolkit. We'll show you the volumetric weight trap, rate comparison tables, and a step-by-step method to get the real forwarder invoice — so you can verify the math yourself in 20 minutes.

Why Shipping Is the #1 Hidden Profit Source

There's a reason agents mark up shipping more aggressively than products: you'll rarely cross-check it. Here's the incentive math:

  • Product markup: Agents usually disclose a 5–10% commission on product cost (or they should). You can easily cross-check product prices on 1688.
  • Shipping markup: Agents never disclose this. They simply bundle it with the product quote and hope you don't ask.
  • The freight forwarder's discount: Forwarders give agents 40–60% off retail DHL/FedEx rates because agents ship volume. Agents charge retail or near-retail rates and keep the spread.
The rule of thumb: If your agent charges "retail-ish" freight rates and refuses to show the forwarder's invoice, they are making more money on shipping than on the product.

The Audit Step-by-Step

This process takes 15–20 minutes and will tell you exactly whether your agent is overcharging.

Step 1: Get the Exact Physical Weight

Ask your agent to send a photograph of the parcel on a digital scale with the exact kg reading visible. Not a "declared weight" in an email — a photo. If the goods are still at the factory, ask for a photo from the factory's warehouse scale.

Tip: Factory scale photos often show the factory name in the background or a timestamp. Agents almost never edit these because it requires Photoshop skill they don't have.

Step 2: Measure the Dimensions Yourself

Ask for a photo of the parcel with a tape measure showing length, width, and height. For small goods in a carton, you can also:

  1. Ask the factory directly (they have these on file for 1688 listings)
  2. Look up the 1688 product listing — most factories post carton dimensions under "packing details" (包装信息)

Step 3: Calculate Volumetric Weight

This is where 80% of the markup happens. Carriers charge on the greater of physical weight or volumetric weight. The volumetric weight formula varies by carrier:

CarrierVolumetric Formula (cm)Divisor
DHL ExpressL × W × H ÷ 50005000
FedEx ExpressL × W × H ÷ 50005000
UPS ExpressL × W × H ÷ 50005000
EMSL × W × H ÷ 60006000
Air freight (LCL)L × W × H ÷ 60006000
Sea freight (FCL)Not volumetric — per containerN/A

Example: A 32×28×45 cm box with physical weight 4.2 kg. Volumetric: 32 × 28 × 45 ÷ 5000 = 8.06 kg. The carrier charges for 8.06 kg, not 4.2 kg. This is why "cheap and light" products can end up expensive to ship.

Step 5: Get the Forwarder's Original Invoice

This is the smoking gun. Every legitimate forwarder issues a digital invoice that includes:

  • Exact sender and receiver addresses
  • Total weight (physical + volumetric, both listed)
  • Carrier and service type (e.g., DHL Express Priority)
  • Tracking number
  • Forwarder's cost — the dollar amount the agent actually paid

Say this to your agent: "Please forward me the PDF invoice from the actual forwarder that shows the carrier and cost — I want to reconcile the total before payment." Legitimate agents have this ready. If they stall or say "we don't share that," you have found the markup.

5 Red Flags in Shipping Invoices

  1. "Special line rate" or "private channel" — These are made-up terms. There are no private express channels; all express goes through DHL/FedEx/UPS. Agents use this phrase to avoid quoting a verifiable carrier rate.
  2. No forwarder name listed — If you can't Google the forwarder, it doesn't exist. Every real forwarder has a domain and location.
  3. Weight is "approximately" or a clean number — Real weights are 32.4 kg, 31.87 kg. Agents round up or down to hide the volumetric calculation.
  4. Shipping is 40%+ of the product value — If freight costs more than 40% of the goods, you're either buying the wrong logistics method or being gouged. For most products under $10/unit, express should be 15–25% of product value.
  5. Separate "handling" or "warehouse fee" that isn't itemized — Yiwu consolidation warehouses charge $20–$30 per pallet. If you see $150 "handling fee" on a small parcel, that's a product markup dressed up as logistics.

Forwarder Relationship Strategy

Once you audit and discover the markup, you have three options:

  • Negotiate it down. Tell your agent you know the forwarder rate is $800 and you'll pay $900 (fair 12.5% margin for their work). If they refuse, find another agent or go direct.
  • Use your own forwarder. Most agents will let you specify your forwarder (try Flexport, Freightos, or a Yiwu-based forwarder like Cainiao). The agent still handles the factory side; your forwarder handles the logistics side.
  • Skip the agent entirely for logistics. Use 1688's built-in logistics tab — most suppliers offer direct air/sea freight quotes at near-forwarder rates. You'll still need an agent for customs clearance on your side, but the shipping portion will be transparent.

FAQ

What's a reasonable agent margin on shipping?
10–20% above their actual forwarder cost is fair for express shipments. That covers the consolidation, labeling, and paperwork work. Anything over 30% — or refusal to show the forwarder invoice — is excessive.
Can I get the forwarder invoice if the agent refuses?
Yes. Once the shipment goes out, the tracking number is public. Contact the carrier directly with the tracking number and ask for the billing summary. DHL/FedEx will release this to the sender or receiver. The forwarder's name and fee are on record.
Why do agents use "special lines" that cost more?
They don't — they call normal express "special" to justify a higher rate. There is no legitimate "private" or "special" air channel from Yiwu. All express freight consolidates through DHL/FedEx/UPS hubs. If an agent can't name the carrier, they're marking up.
How do volumetric weight traps work for sea freight?
Sea freight doesn't use volumetric weight for FCL (full container) — you pay per container. For LCL (less than container), it uses the 1 CBM = 1 ton rule, whichever is higher. So a 10 CBM shipment of feathers (light) gets charged for 10 CBM, not 500 kg.
